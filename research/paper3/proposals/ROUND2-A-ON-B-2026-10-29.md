# Round 2: Expert A on Expert B

## 0. Summary in eight lines

1. **B's central proposition P1 does not hold for B's own negatives.** "Generative and NCE losses share a minimiser"
   needs negatives drawn independently of the positive (from the caption marginal). B's negatives are rule edits *of the
   positive* (swap, replace, add). Then the Bayes-optimal critic contains a term in the edit generator's density, which the
   model can learn without looking at the image (for add-type edits the positive is always the shorter caption).
   P1 then holds only for random negatives, which B's own P2 says carry no gradient.
2. **My round-1 SCG has a symmetric flaw, which I concede.** Its text-direction loss with the log-proposal correction is,
   at beta = 1, a sampled-softmax Monte Carlo estimate of the *maximum-likelihood* gradient on the masked span (Jean et al.,
   arXiv:1412.2007 [A]). The "shared minimiser" is true but empty: an MDM already computes the full softmax over the span.
3. **The merge fixes both.** Score caption candidates in the *image direction* (B's idea: log p_theta(image | caption)), and
   draw the candidates as *span fills from the model's own caption-only conditional* (my idea). Then the caption prior cancels
   exactly (M2): the Bayes-optimal critic is log P(image | caption) alone. Training it with a softmax over the candidates is
   conditional maximum likelihood (CML, the MMI training of speech recognition) of the image-given-caption model. That is not
   MLE, it is consistent, and it is backbone-agnostic.
4. Name used below: **BP-CML** (blind-proposal conditional maximum likelihood). Model: UniDisc 1.4B + LoRA trained with
   BP-CML. Backbones: UniDisc (masked discrete, main), Janus-1.3B (autoregressive unified), and a second masked discrete model
   (Muddit or Omni-Diffusion 8B) if the P0 checks pass; SD 2.1 + HardNeg-DiffusionITM as the closest-prior baseline.
5. Propositions: M1-M3 proven (elementary, and probably known: they go in as lemmas, not as contributions, until the
   novelty-auditor says otherwise). M4 (text direction reduces to MLE) proven. M5 (CML beats MLE in error rate under
   misspecification) conjectured; supported by classic results, to be tested, not claimed.

   512-px confirmations and the second masked model (USD 100-160 at prices I did not check; cap USD 300).

## 1. Strongest critique of B's CB-MDM

### 1.1 Mathematics

**(a) P1 is false for edited negatives.** Let (v, c) ~ P and c^-_k ~ q(. | c^+) (an edit of the positive). Given the
unordered candidate set and the image, the posterior that candidate i is the positive is

  P(i | set, v)  proportional to  P(c_i | v) * prod_{k != i} q(c_k | c_i).

The product depends on i unless q(c_k | c_i) is symmetric in its arguments and uniform over the edit set. For "add object"
edits q(c^+ | c^-) = 0 (the generator only adds), so the posterior is 1 on the shorter caption whatever the image shows;
for "replace" edits drawn from finite cohyponym lists the asymmetry is smaller but non-zero; swaps of two nouns are
symmetric. So the minimiser of B's L_NCE is not the minimiser of L_MDM, and a model can lower L_NCE by learning edit
artefacts. In the image direction those artefacts can only be expressed by distorting p_theta(x^I | c) for edit-looking
captions, which is exactly the generation damage B's K2 guards against. B's P1 cites Poole et al. (arXiv:1905.06922 [A]) and
CPC; their result is for negatives i.i.d. from the marginal p(c) and independent of the positive. With marginal negatives,
P1 holds, but those are B's "random negatives", which B's P2 shows carry almost no gradient. P1 and P2 cannot both apply to
the same training set.

**(b) Length normalisation breaks the likelihood ratio.** B scores s = -NELBO / N_T and -NELBO / N_I. For candidates of
different token length (SugarCrepe add_obj negatives are about 4.5 tokens longer, [L] HARDNEG-FEASIBILITY section 4),
dividing by length is not a log-likelihood ratio, so P1 fails again even with good negatives. The test-time length rule must
be preregistered and justified, and training should use unnormalised log-likelihoods.

**(c) Trainable language prior in the text direction.** s^{->T} = [l(c | blank) - l(c | x^I)] / N_T is computed with the
trained model on both terms. The loss can be lowered by degrading the caption-only model on true captions (raising their
PMI) rather than by grounding. B lists the reference-anchored variant only as an ablation; it must be the default, or the text
direction must be dropped (see M4: in the text direction a correctly referenced contrast adds little beyond MLE anyway).

**(d) P2 is a standard remark, and its link to Paper 2 is a category error.** The Chebyshev split is correct
(E sigma(-beta Delta) <= 4v/mu^2 + exp(-beta mu / 2)), but it bounds the weight on the gradient, not the gradient, and it is the
usual hard-negative motivation (Robinson et al., arXiv:2010.04592 [T]). Paper 2's null concerned an *evaluation-time* gate on
a frozen model; P2 is about *training*. "Explains the Paper 2 null from the training side" will not survive a reviewer.
Keep P2 as a remark.

**(e) P3 (common random numbers) is an identity.** Var(a - b) = Var a + Var b - 2 Cov(a, b). Sharing masks across candidates
is good practice and VRPO (arXiv:2505.19223 [T]) already shares randomness between ELBO estimates; it is an implementation
choice to measure, not a proposition.

**(f) Co-MDM's coherence term measures ELBO gaps, not incoherence.** kappa = N_{T->I} - N_{I->T} is a difference of two
upper bounds with different Jensen gaps. A model with exact conditionals has kappa != 0 whenever the gaps differ, and the
regulariser can be satisfied by shrinking both PMIs (ignoring the other modality), as B notes. Useful as a diagnostic; risky as
a training term.

### 1.3 Feasibility

- **Pass count undercounted.** B counts 5 sequences per triple (joint generative, 2 image-direction, 2 text-direction).
  The text-direction PMI needs l(c | blank) for both captions as well: 7 passes, 4 of them through the trained model (or 2
  extra no-grad passes through a frozen reference). At 512 px with F = 2 N T = 2 x 1.4e9 x 1,100 = 3.1e12 FLOP and about
  2.5 F per LoRA grad pass: 5 grad passes + 2 no-grad = 14.5 F = 4.5e13 FLOP per triple, i.e. 1.8 s compute at an assumed
  25 TFLOP/s and about 3.6 s wall with the duty cycle. 25k triples: about 25 h wall per run, not 10-14 h.
- **"0.10 s per network call"** comes from a sampling run [B]; whether it included the CFG pair is unchecked (B says so).
  It implies about 31 TFLOP/s, consistent with my 25 TFLOP/s assumption, so the gap is the pass count, not the speed.
- **Janus as an "exact two-direction" counterpart.** Janus (arXiv:2410.13848 [A]) decouples visual encoding: text-given-image
  uses the understanding encoder (SigLIP per its paper, [T]), image-given-text uses the VQ generation path. The two directions
  read different image representations, so "matched" means "same objective, different model family", not a controlled
  comparison. Its model licence (DeepSeek model licence, [T]) must be checked for research fine-tuning and release.
- **SD 2.1 + DiffusionITM evaluation cost** is not budgeted: DiffusionITM averages the denoising error over many noise
  draws per (image, caption); with 7.5K rows x 2 captions this can be tens of GPU-hours per model. Unverified; measure.
- **FID-5k** needs 5,000 samples per model (about 9 h at 6.6 s per image [B]); unbudgeted. Drop FID or budget it.

## 3. Merged design: BP-CML

### 3.1 Objective

Data (v, c) from COCO train2017. Sample a span S of the caption (1 to 3 tokens; noun, adjective, relation or random). In one
**no-grad** pass of the frozen model p_0 with the image fully masked and S masked, read r(. | c_{-S}) = p_0(c_S | c_{-S}, blank)
and draw K fills a_1..a_K (one step, factorised); a_0 = c_S. Build candidates c^(k) = (c_{-S}, a_k). Score each candidate in the
**image direction** with the trainable model and common random numbers:

  h_theta(c^(k)) = - NELBO_theta(v | c^(k); t, M)      with the same (t, M) for all k                                (1)

  L_CML = - log [ exp(beta h_theta(c^(0))) / sum_{k=0..K} exp(beta h_theta(c^(k))) ]                               (2)

  L = L_CML + lambda * L_MDM(v, c)                                                                                  (3)

L_MDM is the backbone's own generative loss on the positive pair (UniDisc masked ELBO; Janus next-token loss). No length
normalisation in (1): all candidates condition the same image, and the image length is fixed. Identical fills (a_k = a_0)
are kept (zero gradient). Optional hardness dial: draw fills from r_gamma proportional to p_0(. | c_{-S}, blank)^(1-gamma)
p_0(. | c_{-S}, v)^gamma and add the correction log p_0(a_k | c_{-S}, blank) - log r_gamma(a_k) to h (exact by M1).

Inference (matching): argmax over candidates of h_theta with M = 4 fixed CRN mask draws (deterministic partition of the image
tokens into 4 groups). Generation: unchanged sampler, so any change comes from the weights.

### 3.2 Propositions (state, status)

**M1 (proposal-corrected posterior). Proven (elementary). Probably known** (conditional NCE, arXiv:1806.03664 [A]; logQ
correction, arXiv:1412.2007 [A]); present as a lemma unless the auditor finds otherwise. Let (v, c) ~ P, a span S, and
candidates a_1..a_K drawn i.i.d. from a proposal r(. | c_{-S}, v) whose law does not depend on the true fill a_0 = c_S. Then
P(candidate i is the true fill | set, v, c_{-S}) = softmax_i [ log P(a_i | c_{-S}, v) - log r(a_i | c_{-S}, v) ].
Proof: the joint density of (true index i, candidate values) is (1/(K+1)) P(a_i | c_{-S}, v) prod_{k != i} r(a_k | c_{-S}, v);
divide by prod_k r(a_k | .). Edited negatives (B) violate the hypothesis, because the generator conditions on a_0.

**M2 (prior cancellation and blind-proof pairs). Proven.** If r(. | c_{-S}) = P(c_S | c_{-S}) (the true caption-only
conditional, image ignored), then by Bayes P(a | c_{-S}, v) / P(a | c_{-S}) = P(v | c_{-S}, a) / P(v | c_{-S}), so
P(i | set, v) = softmax_i log P(v | c^(i)): **the image-direction likelihood alone is Bayes-optimal; no caption prior, no
PMI.** Second part: without v, the candidates (a_0, a_1, ..., a_K) are exchangeable given c_{-S} (a_0 is marginally a draw from
P(c_S | c_{-S}) = r), so every image-free score has top-1 accuracy exactly 1/(K+1) (mid-rank ties). Third part (r = p_0, not
the truth; K = 1): for any image-free score, |accuracy - 1/2| <= TV(P(. | c_{-S}), p_0(. | c_{-S})) averaged over c_{-S}
(the pair law is P x r against r x P; triangle inequality through r x r). Standard two-point argument; no novelty claimed for
the bound, only for its use to certify the training pairs.

**M2' (why order and edit negatives are text-hackable). Proven.** For orbit negatives (all permutations of the span tokens,
proposal uniform on the orbit), M1 gives P(i | set, v) = softmax_i log P(c^(i) | v) = softmax_i [log P(v | c^(i)) +
log P(c^(i))]: the caption prior is part of the Bayes critic, so a text-only model is legitimately above chance. This explains,
inside one framework, the blind-model successes on order benchmarks reported by Lin et al. (arXiv:2306.01879 [A]) and the
motivation for SugarCrepe's adversarial refinement (arXiv:2306.14610 [L]). For one-directional edits (add), no image-only
critic is Bayes-optimal (section 1.1(a)).

**M3 (consistency of BP-CML). Proven under correct specification and exact likelihoods.** Under M2's hypothesis, the true
P(v | c) minimises the population L_CML (it attains the true posterior, and log-loss is proper), and it also minimises L_MDM, so
(3) has the true model as a minimiser for every lambda >= 0. With NELBOs in (1), the critic is shifted by the ELBO gap
G(v, c) = NELBO - (-log p(v | c)); the minimiser is unchanged only if G does not vary across candidates. **Conjecture**: for
span-level edits of a long image sequence the gap varies little across candidates; measurable in P0 by comparing h over
several mask draws (variance of the gap difference), not provable in general.

**M4 (the text direction reduces to MLE). Proven.** With critic g(a) = log p_theta(a | c_{-S}, v) - log r(a | c_{-S}) and
beta = 1, the expected gradient of the listwise loss converges, as K grows, to the gradient of -log p_theta(c_S | c_{-S}, v),
the MLE gradient on the span (sampled softmax with importance correction). Since an MDM computes that softmax exactly, a
text-direction contrast of this kind adds nothing in the population and only noise and a temperature in practice. This is why
my round-1 SCG is demoted to an ablation, and why CML must run in the image direction, where the normaliser is over images
and the contrast over captions is not an MLE quantity.

**M5 (CML vs MLE under misspecification). Conjecture, to be tested.** At the population optimum within a restricted class
(LoRA rank r), CML has candidate-posterior log-loss no larger than MLE's (true by definition of the CML optimum); the claim
that matters, lower *error rate* on held-out hard negatives, is not implied (log-loss is a surrogate). Classic support:
Nadas (1983, IEEE Trans. ASSP) and Bahl et al. (1986, ICASSP) on MMI vs ML training; Ng and Jordan (2001, NeurIPS) on
generative vs discriminative classifiers. All three [T], not re-read, DOIs to be verified.

**Remarks (not propositions):** B's P2 (gradient weight bound) explains why blind fills that are trivially wrong for the
image add little; the gamma dial addresses it. CRN variance is measured, not claimed.

### 3.7 HPO protocol

Per backbone, Sobol sampling with ASHA on training examples (2.5K -> 7.5K -> 20K, eta = 3), Optuna storage on disk. Fidelity
check first: 6 configurations at full fidelity; Spearman rho between 2.5K and 20K dev accuracy >= 0.6, else 12 random trials
at full fidelity. Shared hyperparameters (lr log-U[2e-5, 5e-4], LoRA rank {8, 16, 32}, depth {8, 12, all}) searched once on the
FT-pos and BP-CML arms with 24 trials each; arm-specific ones (beta log-U[0.1, 2], lambda log-U[0.1, 3], K, gamma; CB-rule's
lambda_I, lambda_T and debias delta; HardNeg's clip lambda) get 12 trials each, the same for every arm. Fixed: AdamW, cosine with
5 % warmup, clip 1.0, bf16, batch 8 (accumulation if memory forces it; batch never changed without retuning lr). Tuning metric:
dev accuracy (different generator). Edge optima trigger one range extension, reported. Search seed 0; final seeds 1-5 for
FT-pos, CB-rule and BP-CML on UniDisc, 3 seeds elsewhere (disclosed). Tests never touched before the frozen final evaluation.
Primary endpoint (statistician to freeze): SugarCrepe macro accuracy over the 7 subsets, BP-CML vs the better of FT-pos and
CB-rule, UniDisc; secondary: same on Janus, Winoground, What'sUp, blind-proof set, GenEval paired no-harm margin. Uncertainty
by hierarchical bootstrap (images, then seeds); paired per-image tests; Holm over the primary family; per-subset results always
shown (a macro mean must not hide subset losses).

### 3.10 Kill criteria after P0

| id | criterion | action |
|---|---|---|
| K-fid | Spearman < 0.6 between low and full fidelity | random search at full fidelity, fewer trials, same for all arms |

## 4. Questions for the novelty-auditor and the red-team

### Novelty-auditor

1. Is there prior work training a generative vision-language model (diffusion, masked or AR) by conditional likelihood / MMI
   over caption candidates drawn from a density-known proposal, especially the model's own caption-only conditional, with the
   proposal correction? Search terms: "conditional maximum likelihood" or "maximum mutual information" with "image-text",
   "diffusion", "generative classifier"; "self-generated negatives" with "likelihood ratio"; "proposal correction" with
   "hard negatives".
2. Are M1, M2 and M2' known (conditional NCE arXiv:1806.03664; ranking NCE; sampled softmax arXiv:1412.2007; Jean et al.)?
   I expect M1 known and M2' possibly new as a framing. Rule: whatever is known goes in as a cited lemma.
3. Full reads of: HardNeg-DiffusionITM (2305.16397: loss, negative types, any text direction, released code, evaluation
   cost), DPT (2403.04321: adapter score, generation claim), MDC (2510.26799: any matching evaluation), VRPO (2505.19223:
   shared randomness), Clark and Jaini (2303.15233), and 2026 post-training of MMaDA, LaViDa-O, Lumina-DiMOO, Muddit,
   Co-GRPO (2512.22288), "Unified Multimodal Models as Auto-Encoders" (2509.09666).
4. A fresh 2026 search for hard-negative or contrastive post-training of unified discrete diffusion models (B's and my
   searches were search-level only).
5. Verify the classic MMI/CML references (Nadas 1983; Bahl et al. 1986; Ng and Jordan 2001) and find TNNLS-published work
   on discriminative training of generative classifiers and on multimodal hard negatives (target-journal citation rule).

### Red-team

1. Break M1-M4: the independence hypothesis of M1 when spans are chosen by POS (does span choice depend on the true fill?);
   M2 with r = p_0 instead of the truth; M3 with NELBOs whose gaps differ by candidate; factorised multi-token fills; the AR
   suffix term for Janus.
2. Is any gain just more COCO fine-tuning (FT-pos) or caption memorisation? Is the K3 control strong enough?
3. Span-replacement training vs SugarCrepe's swap and add subsets: transfer or not? Does the macro endpoint hide losses?
4. Test-time score: image-direction likelihood is Bayes-optimal for *our* candidates (M2) but SugarCrepe's generator is
   unknown and asymmetric; is the preregistered score still the right one?
5. Fairness: are CB-rule, HardNeg port and the Janus arms tuned with the same budget, and is the SD 2.1 reproduction honest
   about its cost limits?
6. Paper 2 overlap: no Paper 2 family H rows or M4 outcomes enter Paper 3's test; P0a reads Paper 2 arrays post hoc only
   for a cost decision (check the AB.1-type rules allow this or drop P0a).
7. Hardware: whole-GPU memory spikes (two crashes at full memory with a cap in place); is the 9 GB gate enough for training?
