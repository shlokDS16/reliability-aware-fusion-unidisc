## 1. Strongest critique of SCG

### 1.1 Mathematics
1. **Prop. A1 is ranking NCE, not a new result.** With negatives $a_k\sim q$ and critic $\log p-\log q$, the posterior over
   which candidate is real is the softmax of $\log P(a_i\mid\cdot,v)-\log q(a_i\mid\cdot)$. This is the consistency
   statement of ranking noise-contrastive estimation for conditional models (Ma and Collins, arXiv 1809.01812) and the
   InfoNCE optimal-critic result (Poole et al. 1905.06922). A1 is still useful, but as an application: it must be cited as such or
   a TNNLS reviewer will call it overclaimed.
2. **The shared minimiser fails for spans longer than one token, and not only "for the factorised surrogate".** For
   $|S|>1$ the positive comes from the joint $P(t_S\mid t_{-S},v)$, while the negatives come from the factorised
   $q_{\rm f}=\prod_{i\in S}q_i$. The Bayes critic is $\log P_{\rm joint}(a\mid v)-\log q_{\rm f}(a)+{\rm const}$. The SCG critic
   $\sum_{i\in S}\log p_\theta(a_i\mid\cdot,v)$ is a sum of per-position marginals and cannot represent a joint log-density.
   Its L_SCG minimiser is therefore generally **not** the true marginal conditional, which is what L_MDM wants: the two
   objectives pull in different directions. So A1's corollary holds only for $|S|=1$. The honest version restricts the
   exact claim to single tokens and calls multi-token spans a heuristic.
3. **Factorised blind fills are not blind-proof (Prop. A2).** A2 needs $q$ to equal the true image-marginal conditional.
   (i) $q=p_0$ is UniDisc's language prior, which is weak: the project saw poor free-form captions (IDEAS.md, red-team change 2).
   The TV bound is therefore loose, and any stronger text-only model can tell human fills from $p_0$ fills, as a
   machine-text detector does. (ii) For $|S|>1$, independent per-position draws produce incoherent fills ("a red blue dog")
   that a joint LM rejects without the image. Prediction (HYP): a 7-8B AR LM, scoring blind, is clearly above chance on SCG
   pairs. A2 is a statement about scores no better than $p_0$, not about "every score that does not read v". The
   proposed "blind-proof benchmark" inherits the same problem with respect to any text model stronger than its generator.
4. **Paper 2 Prop. 1 is withdrawn as known (D0078; D0079: not even cited in Paper 2).** A's sec. 1 and RGA's
   "falsifiable prediction" (sec. 4.4) use it as a design rule. In Paper 3 it can appear only as the known result
   (D0078 names Li et al., arXiv 2102.08504, App. A.3), never as "our Proposition 1".

### 1.2 Are image-blind self-negatives hard enough, or degenerate?
- **Text direction, single noun or adjective:** a blind fill for "a [MASK] sitting on a bench" is "man", "woman",
  "dog" or "bird". These are hard for a text model and **easy for the image**, the opposite of SugarCrepe's hardness.
  Many are synonyms or still true ("man" vs "person"), which makes them false negatives (label noise). By my round-1 P2
  (gradient saturation), easy negatives carry almost no gradient once the model separates them. SCG also has no curriculum:
  $q$ is frozen, so negatives never get harder.
- **Duplicates:** for frequent words $q(a_0)$ is large, so many of the K draws equal the positive. They give no gradient,
  as A says, but they also shrink the effective K.
- **Coverage gap:** a span fill keeps the rest of the caption fixed, so SCG can never produce **swap** negatives (same
  words, exchanged roles; SugarCrepe swap_obj, swap_att) or **add** negatives (inserted objects or attributes). Those are
  three of the seven primary subsets (HARDNEG-FEASIBILITY sec. 1). SCG trains on a subset of the test's axes and is then
  scored on all of them.
- **Image direction is likely degenerate.** Negatives $v_R\sim p_0(v_R\mid v_{-R},\text{caption masked})$ for a 4x4-8x8
  region are mostly semantically equivalent re-textures of the same content. They are VQ codes that are as compatible
  with the caption as the originals, so they are false negatives. The loss then rewards the exact original codes over
  caption-equivalent ones, which is memorisation of texture, not grounding. The population optimum is still the PMI, but
  the finite-sample signal is mostly noise. I would drop $L^{\rm img}_{\rm SCG}$, or keep it only as an ablation.

### 1.3 Novelty threats A did not list
- **AR-VLM preference optimisation with self-made "ungrounded" negatives.** Examples: POVID (2402.11411, listed by A) and,
  from memory and unverified (E0), Bootstrapped Preference Optimization and similar work that generates rejected answers
  from the model with the image weakened or removed. At inference, visual contrastive decoding contrasts with a distorted
  image. "Negatives from the image-blind self" is the masked-diffusion version of this family, and the delta must be
  argued on that ground.
- **Ranking NCE** (1809.01812) for A1, as above. VisualGPTScore (2306.01879) for the blind-prior normalisation.

### 1.4 Feasibility
- A's own arithmetic gives about 190 attended GPU-h at 256 px and about 640 h at 512 px. Under "no unattended GPU" and
  about 6 h a day, 256 px is 4-5 weeks of exclusive use of a GPU that 2-3 other sessions share. 256 px support in UniDisc is
  unverified (both experts flag it).
- Cross-check of A's FLOP model against our only measurement: OURS 6.6 s per 64 calls = 0.10 s per call at 512 px.
  A's F = 3.1e12 FLOP at an assumed 25 TFLOP/s gives 0.12 s. The two agree, so A's per-example figures are plausible,
  provided a call is one sequence.
- The cost claim (Prop. A3, one pass for all K) is true only because the span is masked and scored per position. That is
  the same factorisation that breaks A1 for $|S|>1$, so the efficiency and the exactness trade against each other.

### 1.5 Evaluation
- **Train-test score mismatch:** training scores one masked span with the rest visible; test scores whole captions with
  4 mask groups (A sec. 3.3). The critic is never trained on the statistic it is judged by.
- **The primary contrast against FT-rule is well chosen, but FT-rule must get the same scorer and loss**, so that only the
  generator differs. A says so; keep it.
- **No fine-tuned AR or continuous counterpart.** A lists CLIP, NegCLIP, SigLIP and BLIP as external reference points
  only. A TNNLS reviewer will ask whether the gain belongs to masked diffusion or to the objective. That needs the same
  objective on an AR unified model (Janus 1.3B, 2410.13848) and the closest continuous method (HardNeg-DiffusionITM on
  SD 2.1, 2305.16397).
- **Generation is "no harm" only.** A unified model's selling point is that understanding helps generation; A never tests
  it.

## 3. Merged design I would bet a TNNLS paper on: GC-MDM (Grounded Contrastive Masked Diffusion)

### 3.1 Objective
Notation as in round 1. $p_0$ is the frozen pretrained model, $p_\theta$ is the LoRA model. Three terms:

**(a) Exact single-token span term (from A, restricted to $|S|=1$), text direction.** Mask one content token $t_s$. Read
the frozen blind distribution $q_s=p_0(t_s\mid t_{-s},\text{image masked})$, which is **cached offline once per
(caption, position)**. Draw $K$ negatives from $q_s$ with replacement. Then
$$\mathcal L_{\rm span}=-\log\frac{\exp\big(g_\theta(a_0)\big)}{\sum_{k=0}^{K}\exp\big(g_\theta(a_k)\big)},\quad g_\theta(a)=\log p_\theta(t_s=a\mid t_{-s},x^I)-\log q_s(a).$$

**(b) Whole-caption contrast, image direction, with CRN.** Candidate set $C=\{c^+,c^-_{1..K'}\}$. The $c^-$ come from a
mixture generator: rule-based replace, swap and add (NegCLIP-style, spaCy and WordNet), plus top-ranked self-blind
single-token fills, both passed through a debiasing filter on the frozen blind prior,
$|\ell_0(c^+\mid\varnothing)-\ell_0(c^-\mid\varnothing)|/N_T<\delta$. Score $s_\theta(c)=-\hat\ell_\theta(x^I\mid c)/N_I$, using the
same $t$ and the same image mask for every $c\in C$. Then
$$\mathcal L_{\rm seq}=-\log\frac{\exp(\beta s_\theta(c^+))}{\sum_{c\in C}\exp(\beta s_\theta(c))}.$$
No reference model is needed, because $\ell(x^I\mid\varnothing)$ cancels across $C$.

**(c) Generative anchor:** $\mathcal L_{\rm MDM}$ (UniDisc's own NELBO on clean pairs), every second step.

$$\mathcal L=\lambda_{\rm span}\mathcal L_{\rm span}+\lambda_{\rm seq}\mathcal L_{\rm seq}+\lambda_{\rm MDM}\mathcal L_{\rm MDM}.$$
Inference: the matching score is the sum of z-scored image-direction NELBO-PMI and text-direction PMI with the frozen
blind reference ($M=4$ mask groups, CRN across candidates, blind terms cached). Generation uses the unchanged sampler and
CFG.

Optional (ablation only): the Co-MDM coherence term from my round 1.

### 3.2 Propositions: proven vs conjectured
| # | Statement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Span term: for $|S|=1$, the Bayes-optimal critic is $\log P(t_s\mid t_{-s},v)-\log q_s+{\rm const}$, so $\mathcal L_{\rm span}$ and $\mathcal L_{\rm MDM}$ share the population minimiser | **proven** (application of ranking NCE, 1809.01812; cite it) |
| P1' | For $|S|>1$ with factorised scoring the minimisers differ (section 1.1 point 2) | **proven** (counter-construction; include it, since it justifies $|S|=1$) |
| P2 | Sequence term with exact likelihoods and $\beta=1$: the optimal critic is $\log p(x^I\mid c)+g(x^I)$, so the true conditional is optimal (Poole et al.) | **proven**. With NELBOs, the excess is bounded by the spread of the order-Jensen gap across captions (ARDM 2110.02037): **proven as a bound**, size unknown |
| P3 | Gradient saturation: $\mathbb E\,\sigma(-\beta\Delta)\le4v/\mu^2+e^{-\beta\mu/2}$, so negatives with large margin-to-noise ratio contribute vanishing gradient | **proven** (elementary). That this explains Paper 2's null is an **interpretation**, not a theorem |
| P4 | CRN variance identity for ELBO differences across captions | **proven** (identity); the size of the covariance is **measured** (CPU, from stored arrays) |
| P5 | Blind advantage on span pairs is at most $\mathrm{TV}(p_0,P)$ for scores dominated by $p_0$ | **proven**, but weak in practice; a **diagnostic, not a guarantee** |
| C1 | The image-direction contrast transfers to text-to-image attribute binding and position (GenEval) | **conjecture**, tested as a secondary endpoint |

No proposition reuses Paper 2's withdrawn Prop. 1.

### 3.7 HPO protocol
A's protocol with two changes. The **common** hyperparameters (lr, LoRA rank, depth, $\lambda_{\rm MDM}$) are searched once
on GC-MDM and on FT-pos jointly, 16 Sobol trials with ASHA on examples (2.5K to 7.5K to 20K, eta 3). The
**arm-specific** ones ($\beta$, K, $\lambda$s) get 8 trials per arm. Every fine-tuned arm therefore gets the same number
of trials for what it actually has. The fidelity check is A's (Spearman >= 0.6 at 2.5K vs 20K on 6 configs, else random
search at full fidelity). The tuning metric is dev accuracy on held-out-generator pairs only. Test sets are touched once,
after freezing. Search uses seed 0; final seeds are 1-5.

### 3.8 Compute (E0; A's FLOP model, cross-checked against our 0.10 s per call)

$\mathcal L_{\rm MDM}$ every second step 1.25F = 8.75F = 2.7e13 FLOP. That is 1.1 s compute, or 2.2 s wall at duty
0.5. At 256 px (if supported) it is 0.32 s compute and 0.64 s wall. One 20K-example run takes about 12 h wall at
512 px and about 3.5 h at 256 px. The blind cache is one no-grad pass per (caption, position): about 100K captions x 1

### 3.9 Kill criteria (preregistered)
- **K0 headroom:** zero-shot UniDisc macro accuracy on SugarCrepe >= 0.92, or blind $p_0$ within 0.03 of it on >= 5 of
  7 subsets. Action: switch the primary test to Winoground / What'sUp / SugarCrepe-C; stop if those are at ceiling or at chance.
- **K-G generator:** fewer than 60 % of 100 hand-audited self-blind fills are true negatives. Action: drop the span term (the
  paper becomes CB-MDM with A's controls).
- **K-L pilot:** GC-MDM minus FT-pos on dev is below 3 points. Action: stop.
- **K-R rule:** at the final evaluation, GC-MDM vs FT-rule has an interval containing 0 and a point gain below 1. Action:
  report as a tie; the paper is then an objective-and-theory paper, not "a better model".
- **K-N no harm:** GenEval drops more than 0.02 or text perplexity rises more than 10 %. Action: one retune of
  $\lambda_{\rm MDM}$, then report the trade-off.
- **K-B blind:** the strong blind AR LM beats chance by more than 10 points on our span dev pairs. Action: drop every
  "blind-proof" claim; keep P5 as a bound only.
