# Red-team of the Paper 3 designs (BP-CML and GC-MDM), 2026-10-29

Role note. My rule is "find problems only". The brief explicitly asks for a survival verdict, three changes and a kill pilot
(items 6-7). I give them in sections 6-7 as the orchestrator's request; everything else is criticism only. Ledger: the brief
forbids editing any file other than this one, so **no ledger rows were added**; rows to add are listed in section 9.

Severity scale: **fatal** (blocks the gate until a resolution is in the decision ledger), **major**, **minor**.
For each finding, "Rejects if" is the result that would force rejection of the affected claim.

## 0. Verdict in eight lines

1. **GC-MDM (B's merge) carries a fatal theory error and a fatal shortcut.** Its P2 is labelled "proven" but is false for its
   own rule-edit negatives (A's round-2 critique was not repaired). Its add-type training negatives are always longer, and
   SugarCrepe add_* negatives are longer in 97.6-98.6 % of rows (OURS count): a length rule scores about 0.98 on 2 of 7 subsets.
2. **BP-CML (A's merge) is the better-founded design** but four major holes remain: (i) the image-direction critic is an
   *unnormalised* NELBO, so it can carry a caption-only "machine-fill detector" that M2's blind-proof bound does not cover;
   (ii) M3 is proved for exact NELBOs, not for the one-sample estimator in the loss; (iii) span fills cannot make swap or add
   negatives, so 4 of 7 SugarCrepe subsets (49 % of rows) are untrained axes; (iv) its decisive comparator is missing: the
   text-direction PMI of plain fine-tuning (zero-shot already 0.940 vs 0.783 image direction on random negatives).
3. **Novelty is thinner than either expert says.** Electric (arXiv:2012.08561) already trains a cloze model by NCE with a
   masked-LM noise distribution (= A1/M1/M4 in text); ELECTRA (2003.10555) uses MLM-sampled fills as negatives; BPO (2403.08730)
   bootstraps negatives from the model with distorted images to suppress the pretraining prior; Discffusion (2305.10722)
   fine-tunes a diffusion model discriminatively for matching. None is in either design's prior-work table.
4. **Evaluation is not "conclusive" by TNNLS standards**: one training corpus and one primary benchmark from the same source
   (COCO), one backbone on the laptop, a 1-point decision threshold below the minimum detectable effect (about 2-2.5 points).
5. **Feasibility**: A's memory estimate ignores that K+1 candidate sequences sit in one autograd graph; B's blind cache and
   candidate count are undercounted. Both laptop plans rest on unverified 256-px support.
6. **Survives**: the BP-CML core (image-direction CML with blind single-token fills) as a *conditional* bet, only if the day-1
   pilot (section 7) passes. GC-MDM should not go forward as written; its CRN practice and generation endpoint are not
   contributions by themselves.
7. **Single biggest rejection reason**: BP-CML, "incremental: a classical CML/MMI objective with ELECTRA/BPO-style self-negatives,
   not shown to beat the same model's own text-direction PMI after plain fine-tuning, on one COCO-derived benchmark and one
   backbone". GC-MDM, "the consistency claim is false for the paper's negatives and the gains are confounded with length and
   with training on the test's own edit taxonomy".
8. **Kill pilot**: zero-shot scorer ladder on length-matched SugarCrepe rows plus a 2-seed micro-train with a mismatched-image
   control and FT-pos scored by its best scorer (section 7).

## 2. Mathematics: re-derivation, counterexamples

Notation: v image, c caption, S span, a_0 = c_S true fill, r proposal, p_0 frozen base, p_theta trained model.

### 2.1 BP-CML (A round 2)

**M1 (proposal-corrected posterior).** The algebra is correct *given its hypothesis*: candidates i.i.d. from r(. | c_{-S}, v)
whose law does not depend on a_0. Known result (Ma and Collins, arXiv:1809.01812 [E1, abstract]; Jean et al. 1412.2007;
Electric 2012.08561). A says so. Two counterexamples to the hypothesis in A's own procedure:

- **F-A1 (span selection depends on the true fill).** A samples S "by type: noun, adjective, relation or random". If S is
  chosen because a_0 is a noun, the true-fill law is the truncation P(a | c_{-S}, v, a in Noun), while r = p_0(. | c_{-S}, blank)
  is not truncated. Then P(i | set, v) is proportional to P(a_i | ., v) 1[a_i in Noun] / r(a_i): the Bayes critic contains a
  caption-only indicator, every non-noun blind fill is identifiable without the image, and M2's exchangeability ("a_0 is
  marginally a draw from r") is false. Same for whole-word spans: if S covers one whole word of |S| subword tokens, a_0 is always
  a complete word, while factorised r produces subword fragments ("gir"+"ble"). **major** (theory and blind-proof claim).
  Rejects if: a POS tagger or a word-validity check alone exceeds chance on the training pairs.
- **F-A2 (factorised fills for |S| > 1).** M2 assumes r equals the true caption-only conditional. A factorised one-step draw
  prod_i p_0(a_i | c_{-S}) is not the joint P(c_S | c_{-S}) even if every marginal were exact; a joint text model separates
  "red blue dog" from human fills (B round 2 1.1.3, correct). So M2 holds only for |S| = 1 single-token words. With the Llama-2
  tokenizer many COCO nouns are multi-token, so the exact regime is restricted to frequent short words. **major**.

**M2 (prior cancellation, blind-proof).** Bayes step correct. Third part (|acc - 1/2| <= TV(P, p_0) for K = 1, image-free
scores): correct by the two-point argument; it extends to K > 1 with the same TV. Two problems:

- **F-A3a (the bound does not cover the trained critic).** The bound is about scores that *do not read v*. The trained critic
  h_theta = -NELBO_theta(v | c) reads v *and* c. A critic can reach 1/2 + TV(P, p_0) on artefacts alone while also reading the
  image; above-chance accuracy is therefore **not** evidence of image use (A round 1 sec. 3.4 states the opposite). With
  p_0 a 1.4B model whose free-form captions the project already judged poor (B round 2 1.1.3, citing IDEAS.md), TV may be
  large and is not estimated anywhere in either plan except B's K-B. **major**.
- **F-A3b (the unnormalised critic can express a text-only detector).** Negatives c^(k), k >= 1, never appear in L_MDM. The
  model can raise NELBO_theta(v | c) for *any* caption that looks like a p_0 fill by flattening its image-token predictions
  (or making them order-incoherent, which enlarges the ELBO gap). Per-token conditionals stay normalised; nothing in (3)
  penalises this, because flattening only touches captions that are never positives. This is a caption-only discriminator
  hidden inside the image direction. SugarCrepe's positive is always the original human COCO caption and its negative always a
  machine edit (HARDNEG-FEASIBILITY sec. 1: 7,511 of 7,511 positives are COCO val2017 captions), so an "original vs machine edit"
  detector transfers to the test with no grounding. **major** (the cheapest alternative explanation of any BP-CML gain).
  Rejects if: BP-CML's SugarCrepe accuracy with a *mismatched* image (another val2017 image) exceeds chance by more than about
  3 points, or exceeds FT-pos's mismatched-image accuracy.

**M3 (consistency).** Proved for exact likelihoods and, with a caveat, for exact NELBOs. The loss uses neither.

- **F-A4 (Monte-Carlo estimator inside log-sum-exp).** The training score is h-hat_k = h_k + e_k, one (t, M) draw shared across
  candidates (CRN). Softmax is shift-invariant, so the shared part of e cancels, but the candidate-specific part e_k - e_0 does
  not. E[-h-hat_0 + LSE_k h-hat_k] >= -h_0 + LSE_k h_k by Jensen, and the excess depends on theta through the per-candidate
  variance of the estimator. The population minimiser of the loss actually optimised therefore rewards lower variance of the
  negatives' estimates and is not the M3 minimiser unless that variance is theta-independent. M3's "K-gap" check measures the
  gap of the *expected* NELBO across candidates, not this term. **major** (a "proven" label on an object not used).
  Rejects if: the per-candidate variance of h-hat across mask draws (P0a-type measurement) is comparable to the mean margin.
- **F-A5 (the ELBO gap is trainable, not fixed).** M3's conjecture "the gap varies little across candidates" is about the base
  model; under training the gap for negatives is a free direction (F-A3b). The conjecture can hold at step 0 and fail at the end.
  **major**, tied to F-A3b.

**M2' (orbit and edit negatives).** Correct, and useful: it shows that for SugarCrepe swap_* (same bag of words in 396/666
swap_att and 158/245 swap_obj rows, OURS count) and add_* the caption prior or the generator is part of the Bayes critic. Hence
**F-A6: by A's own M2', the test-time image-direction score is not the Bayes rule on 4 of the 7 primary subsets.** The paper
cannot claim its score is "Bayes-optimal" on its primary endpoint. **major**.

**M4 (text direction = MLE).** Correct (sampled softmax with log-q correction is consistent; as K grows the loss tends to the
span MLE plus constants). But it cuts both ways:

- **F-A7 (the image direction is the Bayes inversion of a quantity the model already estimates directly).** In the large-K limit,
  BP-CML maximises the induced posterior r(a) p_theta(v | c_{-S}, a) / sum_a' r(a') p_theta(v | c_{-S}, a'), i.e. a *generative*
  estimate of P(c_S | v, c_{-S}). The model's text head already estimates the same posterior *discriminatively* by MLE (L_MDM
  includes text-given-image masking). Classical results (Ng and Jordan 2001, cited by A [T]) favour the discriminative route
  asymptotically, and the project's only measurement agrees: text-direction PMI 0.940 vs image direction 0.783 on random
  negatives (IDEAS.md l.71, exploratory, 300 items). BP-CML trains the weaker route to do the stronger route's job. **major**.
  Rejects if: FT-pos scored with frozen-reference text PMI (or the better of PMI and image direction, chosen on dev) is within
  the interval of BP-CML on the primary endpoint.

**M5** is labelled conjecture; fine. A TNNLS reviewer will note that the 1983/1986 MMI references are speech-recognition
results with model-class conditions that are not checked here. **minor**.

**AR suffix term (Janus).** Re-derived: with r = P(a | c_{<S}) the Bayes critic is log P(v | c) + log P(c_{>S} | c_{<S}, a);
A's formula is correct. **F-A8: by that formula the Janus pairs are not blind-proof** (the suffix term is caption-only, and
left-context fills that clash with the suffix are text-detectable). The Janus arm therefore tests a different estimator with a
legitimate text-only component; "the objective transfers to AR" conflates objective and proposal quality. **major** for the
cross-backbone claim.

### 2.2 GC-MDM (B round 2)

- **F-B1 (P2 false for its own sequence-term data). fatal for the theory section.** P2 cites Poole et al. for "optimal critic
  log p(x^I | c) + g(x^I)". That result needs negatives i.i.d. from the caption marginal, independent of c+. GC-MDM's L_seq
  negatives are rule edits of c+ (replace, swap, add), top-ranked blind fills, and one random caption. Counterexample (K' = 1,
  add edit): c- = c+ plus an inserted noun phrase, so q(c+ | c-) = 0. The critic f(c) = -alpha len(c) reaches training accuracy
  1 and loss -> 0 as alpha grows, without the image; the true P(v | c) has strictly higher loss whenever the image does not
  separate the pair perfectly. So the true conditional is not optimal and the generative and discriminative terms do not share
  a minimiser. A raised this in round 2 (1.1(a)); B's merged table still lists P2 as "proven".
  Rejects if: (already) the counterexample stands.
- **F-B2 (length shortcut aligned with the test). fatal for the evaluation of GC-MDM and CB-rule.** OURS count, section 3.1:
  add_obj negative longer in 97.6 % of rows, add_att in 98.6 %. Rule-based add edits in training are longer by construction.
  The debiasing filter compares per-token mean NLL (|l_0(c+) - l_0(c-)| / N_T < delta), which leaves total length untouched.
  Any add_* gain of GC-MDM or CB-rule is uninterpretable, and neither design lists a length-only baseline.
  Rejects if: a "shorter caption wins" scorer is not in the primary table, or GC-MDM's add_* gain is not larger than what a
  length-only logistic score on its own outputs explains.
- **F-B3 (training on the test's taxonomy).** GC-MDM's generator produces exactly SugarCrepe's seven edit types (replace
  obj/att/rel, swap obj/att, add obj/att). "Generator shift (rules vs ChatGPT)" is a style shift, not a task shift; a reviewer
  will call this benchmark-targeted training. **major**.
- **F-B4 (span term is MLE reweighting).** Term (a) is A's round-1 SCG restricted to |S| = 1; by A's M4 it adds nothing in the
  population beyond L_MDM. B's P1 ("shares the population minimiser") is true and empty. **major** (an objective component
  with no stated mechanism).
- **F-B5 (top-ranked fills break the proposal hypothesis).** "Top-ranked self-blind single-token fills" in L_seq are not i.i.d.
  draws; the most probable blind fills are also the most likely synonyms or still-true fills (B's own "man vs person" point,
  round 2 1.2), i.e. false negatives. **minor-major**.
- **F-B6 (train-test score mismatch).** L_seq trains the image-direction score only; inference uses the z-sum of image PMI and a
  frozen-reference text PMI, with z statistics from dev. The trained statistic is not the one evaluated. **minor**.
- **F-B7 (P3, P4 are remarks).** Chebyshev bound on the gradient *weight* and the CRN variance identity; neither is a
  proposition at TNNLS level (A round 2 1.1(d),(e) agree). **minor**.
- **F-B8 (debias filter selects on the positive).** It conditions the negative law on c+ and is adversarial only against p_0;
  a stronger text model (or p_theta after training) can still separate. It reduces, it does not remove, the artefact path.
  **minor**.

## 3. Shortcuts, leakage, contamination

### 3.1 SugarCrepe length and lexical cues (OURS, CPU word count, section-1 files)

| subset | rows | negative longer | negative shorter | equal length | same bag of words |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| add_att | 692 | 682 | 2 | 8 | 0 |
| add_obj | 2,062 | 2,012 | 5 | 45 | 0 |
| replace_att | 788 | 56 | 72 | 660 | 0 |
| replace_obj | 1,652 | 128 | 314 | 1,210 | 0 |
| replace_rel | 1,406 | 408 | 282 | 716 | 0 |
| swap_att | 666 | 41 | 56 | 569 | 396 |
| swap_obj | 245 | 18 | 6 | 221 | 158 |

Word counts by whitespace split, not UniDisc tokens; HARDNEG-FEASIBILITY sec. 4 gives the token-level mean difference (4.5 and
2.5 tokens), consistent. Consequences:
- **S-1 (major, both).** A model-free length rule scores about 0.98 on add_obj and add_att. A 7-subset macro mean lets a
  length-sensitive scorer (in either direction) move 2/7 of the endpoint for non-grounding reasons. Neither design's primary
  endpoint separates length-matched subsets.
- **S-2 (major, BP-CML).** Span fills keep length and word order fixed, so BP-CML never trains on add (length change) or swap
  (order) negatives: 4 of 7 subsets, 3,665 of 7,511 rows (49 %). B said "three of seven"; it is four. Any macro gain must come
  from the replace subsets or from transfer that the theory does not predict.
- **S-3 (major, both).** "Human original vs machine edit" is a perfect label on SugarCrepe (positives are all COCO originals).
  BP-CML trains exactly "human original vs model fill"; GC-MDM trains "human original vs rule edit". Both can learn authenticity
  rather than grounding (F-A3b). SugarCrepe's adversarial refinement was against its own text-only plausibility models, not
  against a detector fine-tuned on COCO captions.
- **S-4 (minor).** Synonym and still-true fills (false negatives) teach annotator word choice given the image. Under M1 this is
  "correct", but it is not the compositional skill SugarCrepe tests. K-G audit thresholds (50 % in A round 1, 60 % in B) are
  not derived from anything.

### 3.2 The model learning the negative generator
- BP-CML: generator = frozen p_0; detector capacity = the whole image-direction pathway (F-A3b). Proposed controls (K3
  caption-only fine-tune, shuffled-image training) do **not** detect a caption-only cue routed through the image pathway: a
  shuffled-image model has no image signal to route, and K3 trains a separate text model. **major**.
- GC-MDM: generator = rules (length, cohyponym lists) plus top blind fills; learnable artefacts listed in F-B2, F-B5.

### 3.3 Contamination
- **C-1 (major, both).** UniDisc's pretraining overlap with COCO val2017 is unmeasured (U8 in every file). Memorised positives
  inflate base accuracy and compress headroom; the membership check A proposes (likelihood of val captions vs paraphrases) is
  not in either pilot.
- **C-2 (major, cross-backbone).** Janus, Show-o and SD 2.1 have different, undocumented COCO exposure. Cross-backbone
  differences confound objective with contamination.
- **C-3 (minor).** COCO captions are formulaic; many SugarCrepe positive strings likely occur verbatim among train2017 captions.
  A lists a caption-string check; B does not. FT-pos sees the same strings, so this mainly inflates all fine-tuned arms equally,
  but it weakens "generalisation" wording.
- **C-4 (minor, cross-paper).** Paper 2 family H reports UniDisc on SugarCrepe; Paper 3 reports the same model on the same
  benchmark as its zero-shot baseline. IEEE "recycling content" disclosure is needed, and B's pilot step 0 reuses family H for a
  Paper 3 decision without saying whether those images then leave the Paper 3 test.

## 6. Survival, mock review, three changes

### 6.1 Mock TNNLS area-chair review, BP-CML (as currently specified)

- **Summary.** LoRA post-training of UniDisc 1.4B so that its image-given-caption likelihood ranks the true caption above span
  fills sampled from the frozen caption-only model; conditional maximum likelihood with a proposal correction; Janus-1.3B and
  possibly an 8B masked model as further backbones; SugarCrepe as primary test.
- **Strengths.** Clean proposal-independence (M1 hypothesis satisfiable); prior cancellation in the image direction (M2) is a
  neat use of a unified model; honest demotion of the text-direction loss (M4); careful controls planned (FT-pos, shuffled
  image, K3); preregistration culture.
- **Weaknesses.** (1) Objective is classical CML/MMI; the text-direction variant is Electric; self-negatives that strip the
  image are BPO-style; the delta is the image direction of a discrete unified model. (2) Not compared with the model's own
  text-direction PMI after plain fine-tuning. (3) Trains replace-type negatives only, evaluated on swap and add too; add subsets
  are solvable by length. (4) "Blind-proof" is a bound on image-free scores, not on the trained critic. (5) One COCO-derived
  benchmark; one backbone at full strength; Winoground too small. (6) M3 proved for exact quantities, not the estimator used.
- **Questions.** Accuracy with mismatched images? Gain on length-matched subsets only? FT-pos at matched FLOPs? TV between p_0
  fills and human fills as measured by a 7B LM?
- **Score / confidence.** 4/10 (weak reject), confidence 4/5. GC-MDM as written: 3/10 (reject), confidence 4/5.
- **The one missing experiment that would cause rejection.** FT-pos scored with its best scorer (text PMI) against BP-CML on
  length-matched hard negatives, with a mismatched-image control on both.

### 6.2 Which design survives
- **GC-MDM: does not survive as written** (F-B1 fatal theory, F-B2 fatal evaluation shortcut, F-B3 taxonomy targeting, F-B4
  empty span term). What it adds that BP-CML lacks (generation as a measured endpoint, AR and continuous counterparts) is not
  sufficient on its own: AR counterpart breaks blind-proofness (F-A8), SD 2.1 evaluation is uncosted.
- **BP-CML: survives conditionally**, as the core. It is the only design whose central proposition's hypothesis can actually be
  met (single-token, POS-independent spans). Its fate depends on F-A3b and F-A7, both testable in one day.

### 6.3 The three changes that most raise acceptance odds (given at the orchestrator's request)
1. **Primary endpoint and comparator.** Primary = length-matched SugarCrepe subsets (replace_*, swap_*) with add_* reported
   separately beside a length-only scorer; the decisive contrast is BP-CML against FT-pos *scored by its dev-selected best
   scorer, including frozen-reference text PMI*, at matched FLOPs as well as matched examples; every trained arm also reported
   with mismatched images and against a strong blind LM. Decision margin set at or above the computed MDE.
2. **Theory scoped to what is true for the estimator used.** Single-token spans sampled independently of the fill (no POS
   selection, or the proposal conditioned on the same selection event); M3 stated for the CRN Monte-Carlo loss (or the gap and
   variance terms measured and bounded); Electric, ELECTRA, ranking NCE, sampled softmax and BPO cited as the sources of M1/M4
   and the self-negative idea.
3. **Breadth that TNNLS requires, decided before HPO.** Secure the cloud yes for a second *pure* masked-discrete backbone and
   for a second, non-COCO training-and-test pairing; without both, the venue should be reconsidered (a TNNLS Brief Paper or a
   lower-tier venue) before compute is spent.
