# A2.3 pipeline audit of family PS (patch, swap), Paper 2 GATE, 2026-10-29

## 4. Why PS was not met (brief item 3; descriptive, post hoc, no p-value enters any claim)

1. **The corruptions barely move Y.** None-arm accuracy: clean 0.880, patch 0.870, swap 0.870. Pairing clean against
   corrupted none-arm decisions: patch changes 5 decisions (5 right to wrong, 0 wrong to right); swap changes 9 (7 right to
   wrong, 2 wrong to right). Share of items whose decision the corruption flips: 0.010 and 0.018.
2. **Even a perfect gate could not reject at n = 500.** With c = 0, the exact two-sided McNemar needs b >= 7 at the first
   Holm step (0.025) and b >= 6 at the second (0.05). A gate that returned every item to its clean decision would give
   patch b 5, c 0, p 0.0625 and swap b 7, c 2, p 0.18. PS1 was therefore unreachable for any gate whose accuracy does not
   exceed the clean accuracy, and PS "met" (both cells) was unreachable.
3. **The changed-code arm is also far from rejection:** patch b 4, c 2, p 0.69 (accuracy 0.874); swap b 7, c 4, p 0.55
   (0.876). GATE-M's Y equals the changed-code arm's Y on 498 of 500 items in each cell (gate vs chg: patch b 0, c 2; swap
   b 1, c 1). On swap GATE-M returns all 7 corruption-flipped items to the right answer and also undoes the 2 lucky flips;
   3 near-zero items go wrong through false flags (items 11, 174, 472). On patch GATE-M returns none of the 5; the
   changed-code arm returns 1. Removing a 64-token block removes the positive evidence that the clean image had there, so
   for items near zero margin removal is not restoration (the v2 7.4 caveat, `arms.py:168`).
4. **Mechanism of the small headroom on Y.** Mean Delta at changed tokens is 0.027 (patch) and 0.015 (swap), against 0.198
   and 0.199 at unchanged tokens (0.210 and 0.197 at the same positions in the clean image). Uniform random codes are about
   equally unlikely under both captions, so they dilute the item mean (mean shift -0.011 patch, -0.027 swap) rather than
   reverse it. The median absolute clean item mean is 0.190; only 2.4 percent (patch) and 3.8 percent (swap) of items have
   an absolute clean mean smaller than the corruption's shift. On the continuous scale the headroom is large (T1 on d: t 15.0
   and 19.3; T2: t 15.9 and 20.0, `analysis.json` `ps.desc`), on the binary Y it is near zero.
5. **The design assumption that failed.** AD.4's power figures assumed discordance pi_d 0.05 to 0.20 (SAP l.983, marked
   ASSUMED), and AD.4 l.982 warned that "the share of tokens changed by the patch as coded may be small, so low power is
   possible". The realised GATE-M-vs-none discordance is 0.008 (patch) and 0.026 (swap).

So PS as preregistered (Y, exact McNemar, n = 500) cannot separate a working pipeline from a broken one on these two
corruptions. The audit's own checks (sections 3.1 to 3.4) are what show the pipeline was not broken in the classes A2.3 names.

## 5. P arms versus PS arms (brief item 4)
Same loop and same functions (`run_gate_m4.py:139-178`): same Lambda, same `image_delta`, `gate_scores`, threshold, jitter rule
(condition index 6 and 7 for patch and swap, regenerated exactly), arm construction and `evidence_from_delta`; same
`report.report_test` with A0 = "gate0.05" against "none" (`analysis_m4.py:94` and `:181`). Differences, all by design:
(a) PS codes come from `apply_token` on the clean codes, with no pixel corruption and no VQ re-encode, so z is the exact
replaced set (P conditions re-encode, z about 984 of 1024 for jpeg10); (b) `pix` is None for token conditions, which affects
only the texture baseline, not Y; (c) no text-direction arms for patch and swap (A3.3 item 2); (d) the PS bootstrap seed is
`seed_for("ps_family")`, which changes only the CIs. No path difference that could produce a PS-only defect.
