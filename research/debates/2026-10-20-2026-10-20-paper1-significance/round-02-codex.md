## POSITION

1. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE — send to review, not accept as publishable yet.** The review’s scope and the value-rule census could justify external review, but the search and screening record does not yet support a claim of comprehensive systematic coverage: 1,242 seed passes were not screened at full text, a sampled exclusion check found missed includes, and the re-check tranche was frozen early. No human validation of screening or extraction further weakens confidence. The biggest issue is whether the evidence base is reliable enough for the paper’s field-level conclusions.

2. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE — rank the value-rule census first, the comparison audit second, the design space third.** The census appears to be the clearest quantified finding, but its importance depends on whether the sample and coding support the claim that headline results across the field commonly use non-plain or unstated value rules. The comparison audit could be more consequential if it shows that widely cited DLM-versus-AR or synergy claims lack matched budgets, variance, or actual ablations—but those extraction results are not yet available. The design space may be useful synthesis, but fitting 36 of 47 models is not by itself evidence of a significant result.

3. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE — cut or qualify claims of comprehensive coverage and causal or empirical confirmation.** The screening freeze, unresolved seed records, and limited extraction cross-checking call for explicit limits on generalization. Section 9 should be framed as a single exploratory result, not validation of the review’s taxonomy: the CLIP result does not confirm it, and the positive control failed. The stated “difference 0.126” should also be reconciled with the reported values (0.180 − 0.051 = 0.129) before publication.

4. **ACCEPT — prioritize cheap analyses of the extracted records.** Report denominators and missingness for each comparison-quality measure; distinguish author-run results from copied results; and cross-tab budget matching, variance reporting, and ablation quality by comparison type. Add sensitivity analyses for the 80-study sample, especially the 20 seed studies, and for partially completed extraction. These could strengthen the paper without new screening or compute, but they cannot repair the coverage gaps.

**Strongest counter-argument:** systematic reviews can make a publishable contribution through a transparent, reproducible synthesis even when their headline result is an evidence-quality audit rather than a new model. A frozen search can still be useful if its scope and omissions are bounded honestly and the conclusions are robust to them.

**Cheapest observation to settle the central disagreement:** complete the already planned extraction, then check whether the leading census and comparison-audit conclusions survive a sensitivity analysis that excludes seed studies and partially extracted records. That would test whether the proposed anchor is a stable result or an artifact of sampling and incomplete coding.

## CONCESSIONS

The value-rule census is the strongest candidate among the results described so far: it has a concrete estimand and a reported coding and adjudication process. The failed positive control also compels treating the Section 9 finding cautiously.

## OPEN DISAGREEMENTS

The transcript does not establish that the census is representative of the field, or that the comparison audit will yield a stronger result. I would not call the paper’s significant result settled yet.
