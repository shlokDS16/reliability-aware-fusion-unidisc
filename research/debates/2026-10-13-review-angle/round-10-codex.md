## Claim-by-claim

1. **ACCEPT, with a limit.** The reported results support a narrow conclusion: this rubric and these extracts did not produce reliable agreement on evaluability, while claim assertion was more reliable overall. The per-type kappas show that even assertion coding was not reliable for every type. This establishes a measurement problem in the pilot, not that the tables themselves lack the controls.

2. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE.** Both raters’ estimates fall below one half, but disagreement on which claims count as evaluable makes that prevalence too rubric-dependent to report as a finding. It is a useful pilot signal, not a prevalence estimate.

3. **ACCEPT, with a safeguard.** Extractive evidence and computed budget fields should make judgments more auditable; human adjudication can then resolve remaining disagreements. But do not repeatedly revise until agreement passes a threshold and then treat the resulting sample as confirmatory. Freeze v0.3 and its scoring rules before rating a fresh, representative sample. Report outcomes for the current pilot separately.

4. **ACCEPT.** Reporting a weak kappa for a judgment that is central to the review would make the prevalence result difficult to interpret. Descriptive treatment for claim types that fail a pre-specified reliability threshold is defensible, provided the threshold and fallback are fixed before the fresh rating.

## Strongest counter-argument

The proposed process still risks tailoring the rubric to the disagreements observed in round 2. Making every “yes” extractive improves traceability, but does not guarantee that the evidence supports the same construct across claim types. A table cell may be easy to identify while the rule for whether it satisfies a claim remains subjective. Nor does a human rating of 30 claims automatically provide an unbiased gold standard: the human’s expertise, blinding, and adjudication rules matter.

## Cheapest settling observation

Freeze v0.3 and its codebook, then have two independent human raters apply it to a fresh stratified sample, blind to the earlier model ratings. Report item-level agreement, kappa with uncertainty, disagreements by claim type, and the adjudication procedure. Publish prevalence only for types that meet the pre-set reliability criterion; label other types descriptive.

## POSITION

Use v0.3 only after freezing it and validating it on a fresh sample. Keep the current rounds as rubric development, not confirmatory evidence. Report prevalence only for claim types whose measurement reliability passes the pre-registered threshold.

## CONCESSIONS

The round-2 results show that a single broad evaluability judgment is not yet reliable enough to support a prevalence claim. Extractive evidence and computed fields are sensible improvements.

## OPEN DISAGREEMENTS

Whether v0.3 will make evaluability judgments reliable across claim types remains unknown. The reported agreement on assertion coding does not resolve that question.

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

A fresh, blinded validation showing reliable agreement under frozen rules would support reporting prevalence for the qualifying claim types.
