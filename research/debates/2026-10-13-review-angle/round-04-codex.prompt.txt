# Claude, round 1: which review to write, and around which conjecture

Context. Two students and a professor, one 12 GB laptop GPU, strong tooling for systematic
reading. Goal: one review article on diffusion language models (masked or discrete diffusion LLMs
such as LLaDA, Dream, MDLM, SEDD) and unified multimodal discrete-diffusion models (MMaDA, UniDisc,
Show-o, FUDOKI, Lumina-DiMOO), aimed at ACM Computing Surveys (35 pages, surveys only, a new result
is in scope only if it organises the material). Narrative surveys already exist: "A Survey on
Diffusion Language Models" (arXiv 2508.10875) and a 2025 survey of discrete diffusion in language
and multimodal models. A later research article will test the review's conjecture experimentally.

My claims, for you to attack:

1. A third narrative taxonomy would be rejected as redundant. The defensible contribution is a
   systematic review: registered search and inclusion protocol, quantitative extraction of every
   included paper's reported numbers and protocols, a reporting audit (seeds, intervals,
   compute-matched baselines, claim-versus-table consistency), and one organising conjecture.

2. Lead conjecture CA, "conditional synergy". In unified understanding-and-generation models,
   cross-task benefit is not a consequence of unification itself. It appears only when both tasks
   read and write one visual token space under one likelihood; it is bounded by the information
   rate of that tokenisation; and at matched backbone size and data, models with decoupled visual
   pathways (Janus-style) are not worse on either task. Evidence without training: meta-regression
   of published understanding scores and generation scores on design factors across all unified
   models found (shared or decoupled visual encoder, discrete or continuous tokens, autoregressive,
   diffusion or hybrid objective), with backbone size and data as covariates; and a census of how
   many synergy claims rest on a task-removal ablation.

3. Alternative CB, "commitment trilemma". A sampler built on a factorised denoiser can have at
   most two of: free generation order, few parallel steps, exact joint sampling. Evidence:
   published speed and quality numbers collapse onto one frontier when expressed per function
   evaluation per token; exact computation on small synthetic distributions.

4. I rank CA above CB because (a) parallel-decoding theory is crowded (ParallelBench 2510.04767,
   total-correlation bounds, "Breaking the Factorization Barrier" 2603.00045), so CB risks being a
   restatement of known theorems, while causal evidence on synergy is sparse; (b) CA hands the
   follow-up research article a clean causal test on a frozen model.

5. A meta-regression over published tables is legitimate evidence for a review-level conjecture
   even though the numbers come from heterogeneous protocols, provided heterogeneity is modelled
   (random effects per benchmark and per paper) and the limitation is stated.

6. Scope should cover both text DLMs and unified multimodal models in one article rather than
   narrowing to multimodal only, because the journal rewards breadth and the multimodal models
   inherit their objective and samplers from the text ones.

Where I am least sure: claim 5 (whether published numbers are comparable enough to regress on),
and whether CA is already an accepted view after benchmarks that test unified models for real
synergy. Tell me where this plan fails.

## Claim-by-claim

1. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE.** A systematic protocol and reporting audit could distinguish this review, but redundancy is not established just by calling existing works “narrative.” Compare their search coverage, extraction, and audit methods against the proposed review before deciding the gap. Also, “every included paper’s reported numbers” needs a defined extraction scope to be feasible and reproducible.

2. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE.** CA is a promising question, but its proposed mechanism bundles several claims: shared visual token space, shared likelihood, a tokenisation information-rate bound, and no task regression from decoupling. The transcript gives no evidence for these links. Published benchmark scores alone would not establish the causal “only when” claim; designs, data, and training differ together. The Janus comparison also needs a precise definition of “not worse” and matched training conditions.

3. **REJECT.** As stated, the trilemma is too broad: “factorised denoiser,” “free generation order,” “few parallel steps,” and “exact joint sampling” are not operationally defined, and no assumptions delimit the sampler class. A counterexample under one plausible interpretation could invalidate the universal “at most two” claim. The proposed frontier plot would describe empirical tradeoffs, not prove the trilemma.

4. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE.** The transcript’s citations and characterisation of the literature are not verified here, so they cannot support the ranking. Even if parallel-decoding theory is crowded, that does not show CA is novel or causally testable with a frozen model. A frozen-model test cannot isolate training-time effects of shared versus decoupled pathways.

5. **REJECT.** Random effects for benchmark and paper do not make heterogeneous scores comparable. They do not correct differences in evaluation protocol, data contamination, prompt or decoding choices, or selective reporting. A regression could be a descriptive map, but treating its coefficients as evidence for CA would overstate what the data support. The review should first assess overlap and comparability, then report associations only where defensible.

6. **NEEDS-EVIDENCE.** Breadth may suit the journal, but covering text DLMs and unified multimodal systems risks two adjacent reviews joined by a weak inheritance claim. The scope should follow a shared analytic question and a demonstrable gap in prior surveys, not presumed journal preference.

## Strongest counter-argument

The main position—lead with CA and use cross-paper meta-regression as review-level evidence—risks making the review’s central conjecture look empirically supported when the available comparisons are confounded. A disciplined systematic map, with CA framed as a hypothesis and the reporting audit as the contribution, is more defensible until comparable evidence exists.

## Cheapest settling observation

Build a small comparison matrix of the closest existing surveys and the proposed review: search protocol, included-paper coverage, quantitative extraction, and reporting-bias audit. It would quickly show whether the systematic-review contribution is genuinely distinct. For CA, first check whether published studies contain any matched shared-versus-decoupled comparisons; if not, the meta-regression cannot settle the causal claim.

## POSITION

Write a systematic review only if the coverage comparison confirms a gap. Frame CA as a conjecture, not a finding.

## CONCESSIONS

The review’s reporting audit could be a real contribution. Text DLMs and multimodal models may belong together if the organizing question genuinely connects them.

## OPEN DISAGREEMENTS

Whether CA is novel and whether published results support a useful quantitative synthesis remain unresolved.

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

A coverage matrix showing prior surveys lack this audit, plus a set of sufficiently matched comparisons that can test CA’s design-level predictions.

## Revised proposal for you to attack
1. The title is now fixed as "...: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis", so the quantitative
   core must be a real meta-analysis. Unit of analysis: the matched within-paper contrast (an
   ablation or controlled comparison where the paper itself holds everything else fixed),
   converted to a standardised effect and pooled across papers per pre-registered question.
2. Candidate questions: (Q1) effect on understanding of adding or removing the generation
   objective; (Q2) shared against decoupled visual encoder; (Q3) quality against number of
   denoising steps within one model; (Q4) remasking on against off; (Q5) reinforcement-learning
   post-training against its own supervised baseline.
3. New conjecture CD, "step-scaling law". Theory gives a sampling error of order 1/T for T
