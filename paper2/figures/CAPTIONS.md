## F2 `F2_pipeline.pdf` (190 mm)
- Label: `fig:pipeline`
- Section: Introduction (framework figure, first page of the method overview) or the head of Section 3 (Method), before `\ssec:notation`.
- Caption: **Overview of GATE.** An image and a caption are tokenised by UniDisc into the observed pair x~ = (t~, v~) (1); here a patch of image tokens is corrupted (vermillion, label Z_j, used only for evaluation, never by the method). Each image token is scored with the caption withheld (2): B_j conditions on the rest of the image outside its group S_k, D_j on nothing, and u_j = -(B_j - D_j) is the suspicion; no candidate caption is seen at this stage (evidence-separation rule). A threshold tau-hat from n_cal clean calibration pairs, set by conformal risk control, bounds the expected false-alarm proportion on clean items at alpha (3); tokens with u_j > tau-hat form the gated set G and the rest the reliable set R. Two arms use the result (4): GATE-M drops the flagged tokens from the evidence sum, GATE-I re-masks them and re-infills them with the caption masked (S_r steps, L draws, optional threshold kappa). The image-direction evidence of each candidate caption c+ and c- is computed with the image fully masked and the caption visible (5); the decision is Y_R = 1[mean over j in R of Delta_j > 0]. Two-way fusion with the text direction and the secondary summaries d_R and z_R are optional. Blue marks the gate and its flags, dashed outlines tokens dropped from the sum; the token colours are the same as in the visible-sets figure. Conceptual drawing; no data. Source: `paper2/figures/src/fig_pipeline.py`; definitions in `paper2/sections/method.tex`.

## F3 `F3_visible_sets.pdf` (190 mm)
- Label: `fig:visible_sets`
- Section: Method, Section 3.2 (masked scores), next to Eq. (levels) and Eq. (pmi).
- Caption: **One primitive, five visible sets.** Every score in the method is the log-probability that the network assigns to an observed token whose position is masked, under a different pattern of visible tokens (rows of the token bar: caption strip above, image grid below; image modality scored, caption the other modality). B_j: the rest of the image outside the scored group S_k(j) visible, caption masked; D_j: everything masked, the position marginal; C_j: image fully masked, caption visible (the decision evidence Lambda^c_j[y], and C_j - D_j); A_j: the rest of the image and the caption visible (control A_j - B_j); re-infill: reliable tokens visible, caption masked, flagged set G sampled (GATE-I). The gate may use only the levels with the caption masked (blue bracket); the tested evidence uses only the levels with the image fully masked (dashed bracket). The identity A_j - D_j = (B_j - D_j) + (A_j - B_j) = (A_j - C_j) + (C_j - D_j) splits the pointwise mutual information into the intra-modal part q_j, which never sees the caption, and the cross-modal part C_j - D_j. The text direction uses the same patterns with the roles of the modalities exchanged. Schematic 6 x 6 image grid (real grid 32 x 32). Conceptual drawing; no data. Source: `paper2/figures/src/fig_visible_sets.py`; definitions in `paper2/sections/method.tex` (Eq. levels, Eq. pmi, Algorithms MaskedScore and ReInfill).

## F4 `F4_detection_map.pdf` (190 mm)
- Label: `fig:detection`
- Section: Results, detection against cheap rivals (`ssec:res_det`); can also be cited from the introduction as the opening failure case.
- Caption: **Detection map: per-item AUROC of each score against the changed-code labels, by corruption** (run `P2-GATE-M1`, file `runs/P2-GATE-M1/analysis.json`, keys `table.*`, `falsifier.per_corruption`, `paired_q_vs_best_cheap`). Markers are the mean over items; bars are 95 % percentile-bootstrap intervals over items (10,000 resamples) and are narrower than the marker for most rows; the numbers at the right are the means (bold: highest in the panel). n = number of items with enough corrupted and clean tokens (200 patch and swap, 197 JPEG, 198 noise); texture needs a pixel image and is not applicable to patch and swap. Blue: the within-modality masked likelihood of UniDisc (q_j and plain B_j; sky blue: TbV-score, non-finetuned). Reddish purple, hollow: cheap rivals, each drawn in the better of its two orientations, chosen on these data (sign oracle); a trailing minus marks the reversed orientation, so rival values are optimistic. Grey: controls (A_j - B_j and random scores); dashed line: chance, 0.5. Under each panel: the paired difference q minus the best rival, with its 95 % interval, where the best rival was selected on the data. The within-modality score separates replaced codes from clean ones for patch and swap and is close to chance for JPEG and noise; a texture rival with its sign chosen on the data scores higher than q on JPEG and noise, and plain B_j is slightly above q on patch and swap. No claim is made that q detects better than the cheap rivals, and the texture result is reported as a label-circularity concern, not as an explanation of the corruption effect. Secondary family S-D (detection), not a test of the gate effect. Source: `paper2/figures/src/fig_detection_map.py`.

## F5 `F5_gating_frontier.pdf` (190 mm)
- Label: `fig:frontier`
- Section: Method, after Eq. (snr) ("Analysis: when does withholding help an additive statistic?"). Theory only; do not place measured points, E-pred cells or PS/P results on it (red-team C.1 M-d; AB.3).
- Caption: **When does hard gating raise the signal-to-noise ratio of an additive evidence sum? (theory, no data).** Under the independent-token model of Eq. (mean) and Eq. (var), with clean tokens flagged with probability alpha, corrupted tokens flagged with probability beta, a corrupted-token mean mu_c = 0 and kappa = rho sigma_c^2 / [(1 - rho)(sigma^2 + mu^2)], gating raises E[S]/sqrt(Var[S]) if and only if beta > beta*(alpha; kappa) = 2 alpha - alpha^2 + alpha(1 - alpha)/kappa (Eq. snr). (a) The region where gating helps lies above each boundary; the regions are nested, the darkest shade being the hardest case (kappa = 0.5) and the boundary for kappa to infinity being the necessary condition beta = 2 alpha - alpha^2 (dashed). A random gate (beta = alpha, grey) never helps; at alpha = 0.05 the flag rate on corrupted tokens must exceed 0.0975 before gating can help at all. (b) Smallest beta that helps as a function of kappa at the three false-alarm levels of the protocol (alpha = 0.01, 0.05, 0.10; thin lines: the necessary condition); the dots mark kappa = alpha/(1 - alpha), below which no beta of at most one helps. The boundary was checked against the closed form in `exp_p2/gate/theory.py` on a grid before drawing. The model is an approximation (neighbouring tokens are correlated, and mu_c need not be zero). Source: `paper2/figures/src/fig_frontier.py`.
