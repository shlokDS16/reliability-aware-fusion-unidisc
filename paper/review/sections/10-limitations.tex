\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

We group the threats to the validity of this review under four headings: coverage of the literature, reliability of selection and extraction, validity of the synthesis, and the census with the preregistered run.

\paragraph{Coverage of the literature.}
The largest threat to coverage is that screening stopped before all records had been assessed (Section~\ref{sec:method-freeze}). In all, 2,020 identified records were not assessed at full text. Among them are 1,242 seed-corpus records that passed title-and-abstract screening and that include widely used base models such as LLaDA, MDLM, Dream and MMaDA. The included studies therefore lean towards recent work: 449 of the 615 are dated 2026. Only 3 of the 47 design-space models are among the included studies. The landmark set of 42 studies, of which 16 were extracted, describes those papers and is kept out of every count for the 615 included studies. The reassessment of title-and-abstract exclusions found eligible studies in 4 of 82 sampled records, so some eligible studies were lost; when screening stopped, 285 of the 715 records flagged for reassessment had been reassessed. Three IEEE Xplore queries could not be exported, 1,413 subscription records without an abstract were not screened, and 208 full texts could not be retrieved. The evidence excludes non-English work and closed models, and reporting bias was not assessed.

\paragraph{Reliability of selection and extraction.}
The two screeners agreed closely at full text (Cohen's $\kappa=0.89$). The first author's independent check, however, agreed with the final decisions at $\kappa=0.50$ on 24 decided records, below the protocol's target of 0.70 and on fewer than its 100 records. Errors at the boundary of the criteria, such as whether a multimodal system counts as one unified model, are therefore likely. Eight of the sampled studies were extracted twice. The two extractions agree on the extracted values but divide the same tables into comparison groups differently (36 of 245 groups, F1 0.29) and agree moderately on claim status ($\kappa=0.44$); Section~\ref{sec:rq} therefore makes statements about studies rather than about pooled comparisons. Twenty-seven records retain an entry that failed the check against the source text, and the estimates without them are in the Supplement (Section~S6).

\paragraph{Validity of the synthesis.}
The verdict thresholds, the estimator and the population for the headline estimates were fixed after the results were known; Section~\ref{sec:rq} shows how the verdicts move under other thresholds and under errors in the strata. Because the protocol was not registered, a reader cannot confirm that the eligibility rules preceded the data. The seed-corpus studies were judged under the census rules and describe the seed corpus only. The design-space vocabulary is inclusive, which limits what its test of fit can show, and 29 of its 47 rows were carried over from earlier readings rather than re-read. The error budget is a bookkeeping device rather than a measured quantity, and several statements about it are checked only on toy models. Compute-matched and sampler-centric studies outside the sample inform the reading of the verdicts but do not enter them.

\paragraph{The census and the preregistered run.}
The census samples screened seed-corpus records that carry an arXiv identifier, not the whole field. The first author's check of 24 papers agrees with it at $\kappa=0.79$, but the adjudicators were not blind to the coders, and the codebook was refined after agreement was computed (Section~\ref{sec:census-limits}). The run of Section~\ref{sec:exp003} uses one checkpoint, one resolution, 138 caption prefixes and one sampler variant. The positive control of its checker failed, so no null result can be read, and CLIP did not confirm the primary result.

\paragraph{Validation that would address the main gap.}
Further validation would apply the protocol's own standard. Two readers, blind to the screening decisions and to each other, would assess at least 100 full-text decisions stratified by decision and would extract 20 studies. Acceptance requires Cohen's $\kappa\ge0.70$ for inclusion and for the direction of effect of each comparison, and a group-matching F1 of at least 0.6; below either threshold, the criteria or the extraction form would be revised and the affected set redone. The author checks reported here are a first step towards this standard.
