\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

We group the threats to the validity of this review under four headings: coverage of the literature, reliability of selection and extraction, validity of the synthesis, and the census with the preregistered run.

\paragraph{Coverage of the literature.}
The largest threat is the point at which screening stopped (Section~\ref{sec:method-freeze}). In all, 2,020 identified records were not assessed at full text, among them 1,242 seed records that passed title-and-abstract screening and include widely used base models such as LLaDA, MDLM, Dream and MMaDA. The included studies therefore lean towards recent work: 449 of the 615 are dated 2026. Only 3 of the 47 design-space models are included studies; the targeted set of 42 landmark studies, of which 16 were extracted, describes those papers and is kept out of every count for the 615. The reassessment of title-and-abstract exclusions found eligible studies in 4 of 82 sampled records, so some eligible studies were lost; 285 of the 715 flagged records had been reassessed when screening stopped. Three IEEE Xplore queries could not be exported, 1,413 subscription records without an abstract were not screened, and 208 full texts could not be retrieved. Non-English work and closed models are outside the evidence, and reporting bias is not assessed.

\paragraph{Reliability of selection and extraction.}
The two screeners agreed closely with each other ($\kappa=0.89$ at full text), but the first author's independent check agreed with the final decisions at $\kappa=0.50$ on 24 decided records, below the protocol's target of 0.70 and on fewer than its 100 records; errors at the boundary of the criteria, such as whether a multimodal system is one unified model, are therefore likely. Extraction was read twice for 8 of the sampled studies. The two readers agree on the extracted values but divide the same tables into comparison groups differently (36 of 245 groups, F1 0.29) and agree only moderately on claim status ($\kappa=0.44$), so Section~\ref{sec:rq} makes statements about studies rather than about pooled comparisons. Twenty-seven records kept an entry that failed the check against the text; the estimates without them are in the Supplement (Section~S6).

\paragraph{Validity of the synthesis.}
The verdict thresholds, the estimator and the primary population were fixed after the results were known; Section~\ref{sec:rq} shows how the verdicts move under other thresholds and under errors in the strata. The protocol was not registered, so a reader cannot confirm that the eligibility rules preceded the data. The seed stratum was judged under the census rules and describes the seed corpus only. The design-space vocabulary is inclusive, and 29 of its 47 rows were transferred from earlier readings rather than re-read. The error budget is a bookkeeping device rather than a measured quantity, and several statements about it are checked only on toy models. Compute-matched and sampler-centric studies outside the sample inform the reading of the verdicts but do not enter them.

\paragraph{The census and the preregistered run.}
The census samples screened seed records with an arXiv identifier, not the field; the first author's check of 24 papers agrees with it at $\kappa=0.79$, but its adjudicators were not blind to the coders, and its codebook was refined after agreement was computed (Section~\ref{sec:census-limits}). The run of Section~\ref{sec:exp003} uses one checkpoint, one resolution, 138 caption prefixes and one sampler variant; the positive control of its checker failed, so no null result can be read, and CLIP did not confirm the primary result.

\paragraph{Closing the validation gap.}
A further validation would apply the protocol's own standard: two readers, blind to the screening decisions and to each other, on at least 100 full-text decisions stratified by decision and on 20 extracted studies, with acceptance at Cohen's $\kappa\ge0.70$ for inclusion and for each direction flag and a group-matching F1 of at least 0.6; below either threshold, the criteria or the extraction form would be revised and the affected set redone. The author checks reported here are a first step towards that standard.
