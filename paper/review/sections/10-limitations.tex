\section{Threats to validity and limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Selection of studies.}
The largest limit is the screening freeze (Section~\ref{sec:method-freeze}). 2,020 identified records were not assessed at full text: 1,242 seed records that passed title and abstract, 430 records of the re-check tranche and 348 positives of a single-screener rescue pass. The seed records are not a random remainder. They come from a curated list and from the first searches, and they hold widely used base models such as LLaDA, MDLM, Dream and MMaDA. The 615 included studies therefore lean towards recent work: 449 of them are dated 2026. % src: prisma-flow.json not_screened_D0051; fig01-prisma.log not_screened_total 2020; analysis/rq1/rq1-counts.json year (2026: 449)
Every count in Section~\ref{sec:rq} describes the included studies, except the seed-stratum column, which describes the sampled seed corpus; none describes the field, and a statement about a named model rests on the studies that report on it. The re-check of title-and-abstract exclusions found inclusions in 4 of 82 sampled records, which means the single-model exclusions lost eligible studies; the tranche that would have recovered them had assessed 285 of its 715 records when screening stopped. % src: analysis/screening/ta-recheck-k.json; prisma-flow.json full_text_R797
Three IEEE Xplore queries could not be exported, two databases capped exports at 1,000 records, 1,413 subscription records without an abstract were not screened, and 208 full texts were not retrieved. % src: prisma-flow.json subscription.ieee_not_exported, automation_excluded_without_abstract_pending; fig01-prisma.log t1_not_retrieved
Full texts are the open-access or publisher versions at retrieval, and differences between preprint and published versions are not measured. Closed models and non-English work are outside the evidence. Reporting bias (positive results reported more often) is not assessed. % src: research/review/fulltext/fetch-log.jsonl; protocol.md section 3 "Version rated"

\paragraph{Screening, extraction and protocol.}
Every screening and extraction decision was made by language models. The two screeners agreed well on the full texts of T1 ($\kappa=0.894$), but no human validation sample was screened, so agreement with a human is unknown (Section~\ref{sec:method-screening}). Four records that screener~B could not read were settled by the first author confirming screener~A's reading. Extraction used one model extractor, with a second on 8 of the 80 sampled studies, so field-level reliability rests on 8 studies. On those 8 the two extractors agree on values, appraisal scores and descriptive study-level flags, but on only 5 of 8 direction flags, and they cut the same tables into different comparison groups (36 of the 245 groups they listed were found by both; F1 0.29, below the plan's warning line of 0.6), and claim status agrees only moderately ($\kappa=0.44$). The number of comparisons a study contributes therefore depends on who read it, so Section~\ref{sec:rq} reports no unit totals; study-level agreement rests on 62 flag pairs from 8 studies, and a second extractor on every study would be needed to measure it well. Twenty-seven records kept at least one entry the validator rejected; the estimates without them are in Supplement~S6. The verdict thresholds, the estimator and the choice of the included studies as the primary population were fixed after the results were known (decision D0060). The seed stratum is reported apart because its 20 studies were judged eligible under the census rules, not the screening criteria, and read from arXiv HTML without page numbers; its estimates describe the seed corpus only, and seed records outside the census pool (the 1,106 that both title-and-abstract screeners passed and that have an arXiv identifier) are outside every stratum. The quality appraisal covers the sample, not every included study. The protocol was not frozen or registered before screening (Section~\ref{sec:method-design}), so the reader cannot check that the eligibility rules preceded the data. % src: agreement-ft-t1.json; decisions D0034, D0041, D0052, D0060; protocol.md header; analysis/extraction/d0052/agreement-8-texts (F1_G numerator 72 = 2 x 36 matched, of 245 listed; falsifier_F1_G_drawn_below_0.6 true; M-CL kappa 0.44); rq-results.json D0060 agreement_extra (flags 55/62), sensitivity (27 rejected); seed-sample.json census_pool 1106 (red-team m3)

\paragraph{The census.}
The census samples records that passed screening and have an arXiv identifier, not the field; no human coded it, its adjudicators were not blind to the coders, and codebook v1.2 was written after agreement was computed (Section~\ref{sec:census-limits}). % src: census-result-v2.md ("Limits"); census-codebook-v1.2.md "Disclosure"

\paragraph{Synthesis.}
The design-space table uses an inclusive vocabulary, and 29 of its 47 rows were copied from earlier reading and not re-read (Section~\ref{sec:design}). The error budget is bookkeeping and not a measured quantity, and several statements about it are checked by exact enumeration on toy models only. % src: design-space-v1.md; framework-v2.md sections B and D

\paragraph{The measurement on a frozen model.}
Section~\ref{sec:exp003} reports one run, on one checkpoint, at one resolution, with 138 prefixes from short COCO captions and a 32-step variant of the official sampler. We did not test other models, other prefixes, other step counts or a second seed set. Presence is judged by one small vision-language checker with no human check of its answers; its positive control failed (PC-1), so no null can be read, and CLIP did not confirm the primary result. Quality-mediated coupling is not excluded, the positive attribution term bundles ordering, schedule and selection, and six deviations from the preregistered plan are listed (Section~\ref{sec:exp003-deviations}). No human reviewed the preregistration, and the novelty check must be repeated before submission. % src: gate audit 2026-10-15-gate-EXP-003.md sections 1.4, 4 and 6; novelty audit section 7; decisions D0017, D0022, D0023, D0025

\paragraph{Use of AI.}
All research steps listed in Table~\ref{tab:ai} used language models, and models of one family did most of the coding, adjudication and drafting. Their errors are therefore likely to be correlated, and the checks recorded for them are mostly checks by other models of the same family.
