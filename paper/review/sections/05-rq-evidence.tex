\section{What the evidence shows, by research question}
\label{sec:rq}

This section answers the six research questions from the 615 included studies. Two kinds of number appear. Counts over all included studies come from fields the full-text screeners recorded (Section~\ref{sec:method-extraction}). Statements about how studies ran their comparisons, and the quality appraisal, come from the stratified sample of 80 studies. They are given as the number of sampled studies and as a share weighted to the size of each stratum, with a 95\% interval. Both the counts over all 615 and the sample estimates describe the included studies. These studies lean towards recent work and leave out most of the seed corpus (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). A sampled stratum of seed-corpus studies is reported in a separate column. No number describes the field as a whole. Table~\ref{tab:rq-summary} gives the answers in brief. % src: analysis/rq1/rq1-counts.json; analysis/extraction/d0052/sample.json; gate-manifest.json frame sizes

\subsection{RQ1: which formulations exist}
\label{sec:rq1}

Of the 615 included studies, 557 qualify through a diffusion model whose output includes text or code (criterion I1(a)), 41 through a unified model that understands and generates across text and another modality (I1(b)), and 17 through both. In 12 of the I1(a) studies no reported result is text: the model generates only non-language strings, and these studies are counted apart. % src: analysis/rq1/rq1-counts.json i1 {"a": 557, "b": 41, "ab": 17}, nonlang_only 12 (after the D0059 field resolutions)
Both screeners recorded the study's modality as text only for 410 studies and as involving image, video, audio, speech or 3D for 164. A further 28 involve other non-text inputs or outputs, and the two screeners recorded 13 differently. % src: rq1-counts.json mm (text 410, multimodal 164, other 28; splits 3 + 2 + 2 + 6 = 13)
The included studies are recent: 449 are dated 2026 and 111 are dated 2025, against 55 for 2021 to 2024 together. Unified I1(b) or I1(a)-and-(b) studies number 36 in 2026, 12 in 2025 and 10 earlier. % src: rq1-counts.json year {2021: 3, 2022: 5, 2023: 13, 2024: 34, 2025: 111, 2026: 449}; i1_by_year (2026 b 25 + ab 11 = 36; 2025 b 8 + ab 4 = 12; earlier b 1 + ab 2 + b 7 = 10)

\paragraph{Population and estimates.} The sample-based statements describe the included studies: 59 sampled from the 584 open to sampling. Shares are weighted to the stratum sizes and carry 95\% bootstrap intervals, with the unweighted count beside each share; directions rest on one extractor's reading and are stated per study (Section~\ref{sec:method-extraction}). Two further columns are reported apart: 20 seed-corpus studies, and 16 of the 42 landmark studies, whose results by question are in the Supplement. % src: rq-results.json D0060 landmark_meta (n_extracted 16, N_includes 42, order_seed 20261024); rq-results.json D0060 (N_primary 584, n_primary 59; N_by_stratum G-long 265, G-short 267, G-mm 52; bootstrap B 4000 seed 20261021); left_frame_D0059 [M18059]; decision D0060
\paragraph{Verdicts.} The protocol names four verdicts without defining them. We define them after the results were known and apply them mechanically to RQ2 to RQ5 on the included studies (Section~\ref{sec:method-deviations}). A verdict is \emph{untested} if fewer than five sampled studies give a signed direction. It is \emph{mixed} if neither direction holds in at least two thirds of them (weighted). It is \emph{weakly supported} if one direction holds in at least two thirds but fewer than half of the studies making the comparison report both its budget and its run-to-run variation. It is \emph{well supported} if one direction holds in at least two thirds and at least half report both. RQ1 and RQ6 describe the evidence and are not graded. The verdicts do not change when the 27 records the validator still rejected are left out, except that RQ5 then has four signed studies and becomes untested. They also do not change when the second extractor's records replace the adjudicated ones (Supplement~S6). Of six alternative thresholds, changed one at a time, two alter a verdict: a direction share of 0.60 makes RQ4 and RQ5 weakly supported, and a minimum of eight signed studies makes RQ5 untested; a direction share of 0.75, a control share of 0.40 or 0.60 and a minimum of three change nothing (Supplement~S6). Changed together over a grid of 60 settings (direction share 0.55 to 0.80, control share 0.30 to 0.60, minimum of 3 to 8 signed studies), the thresholds never yield a well-supported verdict, but the other verdicts move: RQ2 is mixed in all 60 settings, RQ3 weakly supported in 48 and mixed in 12, RQ4 mixed in 36 and weakly supported in 24, and RQ5 mixed in 24, untested in 20 and weakly supported in 16; only 16 settings reproduce all four main verdicts. The verdicts also hold under errors in the stratum design. Merging the two language-only strata changes none; moving each language-only study to the other language stratum with probability 0.2 turns RQ4 weakly supported in 8 of 1,000 draws and changes nothing else; and dropping any one sampled study changes only RQ5, which becomes untested when the dropped study is one of its five signed studies. The Kish effective sample size is 46 for the 59 included studies, and 16, 18, 14 and 5 for the signed studies behind RQ2 to RQ5 (Supplement~S6). Because the thresholds were chosen after the results were seen, the verdicts summarise the sample under a stated rule; they are not tests. % src: decision D0060; rq-results.json D0060 verdicts (included, included_without_rejected, included_EB_on_8); D0060.verdicts_threshold_sensitivity

\paragraph{Formulations in the sample (RQ1).} Absorbing (masked) discrete state spaces are the most common: 27 of 59 included studies, 0.59 [0.45, 0.73] of the included studies. Continuous latent spaces appear in 17, 13 of them unified models. The rule that decides which positions to commit is confidence-ranked in 9 studies and threshold-adaptive in 5, all positions are updated together in 22, and 9 do not state it. Blockwise (semi-autoregressive) generation is used in 12 (0.24 [0.12, 0.38]). Sixteen studies give the sampler no way to revise a committed token; 25 keep no memory across steps and 5 use an exact key--value cache. Eleven are training-free (0.24 [0.12, 0.38]): the contribution is a sampler, cache or guidance rule applied to an existing model. % src: rq-results.json D0060 included RQ1_state_space=discrete-absorbing 27/59 0.594 [0.45, 0.731]; continuous-latent 17 (G-mm 13 in RQ1.state_space by_stratum); position_rule confidence-ranked 9, threshold-adaptive 5, all-positions 22, not_stated 9; blockwise yes 12 0.243 [0.122, 0.377]; revision none 16; memory none 25, exact-cache 5; training_free yes 11 0.237 [0.12, 0.375]
Among the 16 extracted landmark studies, 7 use an absorbing discrete state space and 3 a continuous embedding space; 4 commit positions by confidence, 2 generate blockwise and 2 are training-free. % src: rq-results.json D0060 landmark RQ1_state_space=discrete-absorbing 7/16, continuous-embedding 3/16; RQ1_position_rule=confidence-ranked 4/16; RQ1_blockwise=yes 2/16; RQ1_training_free=yes 2/16

\paragraph{Continuous and flow-based text models.} Two recent continuous models close much of the gap to discrete diffusion: LangFlow, which links embedding-space diffusion to flow matching, reports likelihood and sample quality that rival discrete diffusion~\cite{arXiv:2604.11748}, and CoDAR, which keeps diffusion continuous and rounds to tokens with a contextual autoregressive decoder, reports generation quality competitive with strong discrete models~\cite{arXiv:2603.02547}. Theory compares their parallelism: uniform and Gaussian diffusion can sample in a number of forward passes that scales with the dual total correlation of the data, previously shown only for masked diffusion, and the same work proves separations among the three families~\cite{arXiv:2609.20539}. In the notation, LangFlow and CoDAR change the channel (C1), and CoDAR also the final rounding step.

\subsection{RQ2: diffusion against autoregressive models}
\label{sec:rq2}
Twenty-five of the 59 sampled included studies place a diffusion arm and an autoregressive arm in the same table (0.40 [0.26, 0.54] of the included studies). One of the 25 matches the compute budget of the two arms in at least one such comparison (0.06 [0.00, 0.20]), and none does so in all of them (upper bound 0.18). In 23 of them, the budget of one arm is unreported in at least one comparison (0.93 [0.79, 1.00]). Five state that they copied the autoregressive numbers from another paper rather than running the baseline (0.20 [0.03, 0.40]), and 3 report run-to-run variation for these comparisons (0.18 [0.00, 0.37]). % src: rq-results.json D0060 included RQ2_has_ar 25/59 0.396 [0.255, 0.537]; RQ2_matched_any 1/25 0.06 [0.0, 0.204]; RQ2_matched_all 0/25 [0, 0.18]; RQ2_budget_not_reported_any 23/25 0.928 [0.785, 1.0]; RQ2_copied_stated_any 5/25 0.198 [0.028, 0.397]; RQ2_variance_any 3/25 0.181 [0.0, 0.369]; group_counts_included ar_groups_by_budget {not reported 75, reported 23, matched 3}
The direction is split. Of the 22 studies with a signed contrast, diffusion is better on most of its units in 12 and worse in 8 (weighted 0.45 [0.21, 0.71] and 0.46 [0.21, 0.71]). The comparisons pit the best diffusion arm against the best autoregressive arm with no matching of size or budget, so the split says more about the baselines chosen than about the model families. Seven of the 59 studies claim in the abstract that their diffusion model matches, approaches or beats an autoregressive model (0.15 [0.05, 0.26]). Half of the seed studies make the comparison (10 of 20), and there diffusion is worse in 6 of the 10. % src: D0060 included RQ2_signed 22, RQ2_better_majority 12/22 0.452 [0.212, 0.711], RQ2_worse_majority 8/22 0.461 [0.212, 0.711]; RQ2_abstract_claim 7/59 0.145 [0.048, 0.26]; seed RQ2_has_ar 10/20, RQ2_worse_majority 6/10
\paragraph{Compute-matched studies.} Studies designed to make this comparison at matched compute sharpen the picture without reversing the verdict. Masked diffusion scales at a rate comparable to autoregressive models, with a relatively small compute gap~\cite{arXiv:2410.18514}. A FLOP-matched study of masked, uniform-state and interpolating diffusion finds that a cross-entropy objective makes masked diffusion about 12\% more FLOP-efficient, that perplexity is informative within a diffusion family but can mislead across families, and that, among diffusion families, one with worse likelihood scaling can sit better on the speed--quality Pareto frontier because it samples faster~\cite{arXiv:2602.15014}. When unique data rather than compute is the constraint, the order reverses: with repeated epochs diffusion overtakes autoregressive training beyond a critical compute~\cite{arXiv:2507.15857,arXiv:2511.03276}. These studies point to conditions under which a diffusion model may be preferable (data-constrained pretraining, or deployment in which parallel decoding speed matters), which the best-against-best comparisons of the sample cannot isolate. They also support reading the mixed verdict as a statement about how the sampled studies compare, at unmatched budgets, rather than about the model families.
\textbf{Verdict: mixed} (22 signed studies; neither direction reaches two thirds). The comparison is almost never made at a stated common budget.

\subsection{RQ3: what training strategies contribute}
\label{sec:rq3}
Twenty-seven sampled included studies contrast training choices within the paper (0.44 [0.31, 0.58]). In 18 of the 24 with a signed contrast, the paper's anchor configuration (its focal or default setting, or its first own arm when neither is stated) is better on most units (0.79 [0.59, 0.95]) and in 3 it is worse. % src: D0060 included RQ3_has_training 27/59 0.444 [0.308, 0.583]; RQ3_anchor_better_majority 18/24 0.789 [0.593, 0.951]; RQ3_anchor_worse_majority 3/24; group_counts_included rq3_anchor_rules_signed_groups {focal-label 32, stated-default 21, first-own-arm 42, value-identity 24, other-own-model 2}
The control behind these contrasts is thin. Three of the 27 state for every training contrast what was held fixed (0.11 [0.00, 0.26]), 7 ablate the component they credit (0.28 [0.10, 0.49]), and 4 report run-to-run variation (0.17 [0.01, 0.35]); 3 report both a budget and variation (0.12). % src: D0060 included RQ3_held_fixed_all 3/27 0.113 [0.0, 0.255]; RQ3_credited_ablation_any 7/27 0.282 [0.099, 0.49]; RQ3_variance_any 4/27 0.171 [0.013, 0.351]; RQ3_control 3/27 0.118
\textbf{Verdict: weakly supported.} The direction favours the papers' anchor configurations (the two-thirds threshold is passed), but few contrasts isolate the change and almost none show that the gain exceeds run-to-run variation.

\subsection{RQ4: quality against inference compute}
\label{sec:rq4}
Inference settings are varied in 31 of the 59 sampled included studies (0.65 [0.53, 0.78]), most often the number of steps or network calls (14), guidance (9) and the tokens committed per step (7). Twenty-four of the 31 report the inference budget for at least one such contrast (0.81 [0.66, 0.94]) and 8 report run-to-run variation (0.29 [0.14, 0.46]). % src: D0060 included RQ4_has_inference 31/59 0.651 [0.525, 0.775]; RQ4_budget_reported_any 24/31 0.806 [0.655, 0.939]; RQ4_variance_any 8/31 0.29 [0.135, 0.464]; RQ4.by_factor by_stratum, update strata summed: steps-or-nfe 14, guidance 9, tokens-per-step 7
Fifteen included studies sweep the number of steps or the tokens per step. Read along increasing compute, most of a study's curves improve in 9 of them (0.61 [0.34, 0.87]), and every curve improves in 5 (0.37 [0.12, 0.64]). In 7, at least one curve peaks inside the range or moves both ways (0.47 [0.20, 0.72]); ignoring changes under 1\% does not remove these cases (7 of 13). Two studies supply most of the curves, so we count studies. % src: D0060 included RQ4_has_sweep 15/59; RQ4_sweep_up_majority 9/15 0.609 [0.335, 0.866]; RQ4_sweep_all_up 5/15 0.37 [0.123, 0.643]; RQ4_any_nonmonotone 7/15 0.467 [0.2, 0.72]; RQ4_any_nonmonotone_tol1pct 7/13; group_counts_included sweep_curves_by_study (M15781 62, M17249 60 of 170)
\paragraph{Sampler-centric studies.} Recent work outside the sample shows mechanisms that could produce such curves and offers levers. An oracle study that replaces the learned denoiser with the exact posterior of a hidden Markov model shows that few-step samplers are not distributionally correct even with a perfect denoiser, with transition-level mismatch that vanishes only as the number of steps approaches the sequence length; better NLL, generative perplexity or MAUVE does not imply correct sampling~\cite{arXiv:2602.19619}. Dependency-aware selection (DEMASK) predicts pairwise dependence between masked positions and co-commits only positions with bounded cumulative dependence, which bounds the total-variation gap to the model's joint under a sub-additivity assumption, with a reported 1.7 to 2.2 times speed-up at matched or better accuracy on one 7B model~\cite{arXiv:2604.02560}. A learned commitment controller (TraceLock) replaces hand-set confidence rules with a policy trained on the future stability of decoding traces and transfers across window widths, lengths and step budgets~\cite{arXiv:2605.24697}. Both act on the position rule of C4, not on the network. For revision (C5), a re-evaluation finds that a post-hoc confidence-based remasking method brings little or no benefit over confidence-based unmasking at the usual short block lengths, and partly offsets the errors added by more stochastic decoding~\cite{arXiv:2606.12232}; for that method, the value of remasking depends on stochasticity and block size.
\textbf{Verdict: mixed} (15 sweeping studies; improvement with compute holds in 0.61, short of two thirds). More compute often helps, but a fixed step count or parallelism level is not a safe default. The census shows that even the value rule behind a headline number is often unstated (Section~\ref{sec:census}).

\subsection{RQ5: synergy in unified models}
\label{sec:rq5}
The unified stratum gives 20 sampled studies of 52. Three claim in the abstract that understanding and generation help each other; none of the 20 uses a benchmark built to test that claim (upper bound 0.17 for the 52). Eight contain an ablation that removes a task or modality or compares shared with decoupled parameters, and two of the three claiming studies are among them. Five of the eight give signed results. Read as the joint (shared or fuller) model against the reduced (decoupled) one, the joint model is better on most units in 2 and worse in 3. % src: D0060 included RQ5_claim 3/20; RQ5_benchmark 0/20 [0, 0.168]; RQ5_has_ablation 8/20; RQ5 claim_and_group 2; RQ5_signed 5; RQ5_joint_better_majority 2/5, RQ5_joint_worse_majority 3/5 (orientation by arm: analysis/extraction/d0052/rq5-orientation.jsonl; group_counts_included rq5_signed_by_study)
The abstract claim types come from an earlier rubric in which the synergy claim type did not reach reliability, so the count of three is descriptive (Section~\ref{sec:method-deviations}).
\paragraph{Fully continuous unified models.} A recent unified model, Multimodal Flow, organises text blocks and images as ordered continuous chunks under one chunk-causal flow backbone that learns a single vector field~\cite{arXiv:2609.40362}. A shared objective and sampler across modalities would remove one source of difference between the joint and decoupled arms of a synergy ablation; whether such a model removes the others is open.
\textbf{Verdict: mixed} (five signed studies, split 2 to 3). Synergy is untested on a purpose-built benchmark.

\subsection{How firm the conclusions for RQ2 to RQ5 are}
\label{sec:rq-firmness}
Three features of the evidence limit every verdict above. First, matched compute is rare: only 1 of the 25 sampled studies that compare diffusion with autoregressive models matches the compute budget of the two arms in any comparison, and 23 leave one arm's budget unreported in at least one comparison. The compute-matched studies outside the sample are therefore the better guide to the question behind RQ2. Second, run-to-run variation is seldom reported: the main comparison is repeated over runs in 10 of 54 included studies and in none of the 14 landmark studies scored, so few reported gains are shown to exceed seed-to-seed variation. Third, the RQ5 verdict rests on five signed studies; it becomes untested when any one of them is dropped and in 20 of the 60 threshold settings, and weakly supported in 16, so RQ5 is best read as unresolved. RQ2 is the most stable verdict, mixed in all 60 settings.
