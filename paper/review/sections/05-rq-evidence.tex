\section{What the evidence shows, by research question}
\label{sec:rq}

This section answers the six research questions from the 615 included studies. Two kinds of number appear. Counts over all included studies come from fields the full-text screeners recorded (Section~\ref{sec:method-extraction}). Statements about how studies ran their comparisons, and the quality appraisal, come from the stratified sample of 80 studies; they are given as the number of sampled studies and as a share weighted to the size of each stratum, with a 95\% interval. The counts over all 615 and the sample estimates both describe the included studies, which lean towards recent work and leave out most of the seed corpus (Section~\ref{sec:limits}); a sampled stratum of seed-corpus studies is reported in a separate column, and no number describes the field as a whole. % src: analysis/rq1/rq1-counts.json; analysis/extraction/d0052/sample.json; gate-manifest.json frame sizes

\subsection{RQ1: which formulations exist}
\label{sec:rq1}

Of the 615 included studies, 557 qualify through a diffusion model whose output includes text or code (criterion I1(a)), 41 through a unified model that understands and generates across text and another modality (I1(b)), and 17 through both. In 12 of the I1(a) studies no reported result is text: the model generates only non-language strings, and these studies are counted apart. % src: analysis/rq1/rq1-counts.json i1 {"a": 557, "b": 41, "ab": 17}, nonlang_only 12 (after the D0059 field resolutions)
Both screeners recorded the study's modality as text only for 410 studies and as involving image, video, audio, speech or 3D for 164; 28 involve other non-text inputs or outputs and 13 were recorded differently by the two screeners. % src: rq1-counts.json mm (text 410, multimodal 164, other 28; splits 3 + 2 + 2 + 6 = 13)
The included studies are recent: 449 are dated 2026 and 111 are dated 2025, against 55 for 2021 to 2024 together. Unified I1(b) or I1(a)-and-(b) studies number 36 in 2026, 12 in 2025 and 10 earlier. % src: rq1-counts.json year {2021: 3, 2022: 5, 2023: 13, 2024: 34, 2025: 111, 2026: 449}; i1_by_year (2026 b 25 + ab 11 = 36; 2025 b 8 + ab 4 = 12; earlier b 1 + ab 2 + b 7 = 10)
The screeners also marked which research questions each study carries evidence for, by pointing to a results table or figure. Both screeners marked an autoregressive baseline on the same task in the study's own results (RQ2) for 246 studies, a within-paper contrast of a training choice (RQ3) for 237, a within-paper contrast of an inference setting (RQ4) for 394, and, among unified studies, a synergy ablation, benchmark or claim (RQ5) for 32. % src: rq1-counts.json rq_both {"RQ2": 246, "RQ3": 237, "RQ4": 394, "RQ5": 32}; criteria-v1.6-screener.md "RQ TAGS"

\paragraph{Population and estimates.} The sample-based statements describe the included studies: 59 sampled from the three strata of the 584 studies that full-text screening included and that were open to sampling (19 long and 20 short language-only studies and 20 unified studies; one sampled study left the frame when its screening fields were resolved). Shares are weighted to the stratum sizes (265, 267 and 52) and given with a 95\% interval from a bootstrap over studies within strata; the unweighted count is given beside each share. The 20 seed-corpus studies are reported in a separate column: they were judged eligible under the census rules, not under the screening criteria, and read from arXiv HTML text, so they describe the seed corpus and not the included studies. % src: rq-results.json D0060 (N_primary 584, n_primary 59; N_by_stratum G-long 265, G-short 267, G-mm 52; bootstrap B 4000 seed 20261021); left_frame_D0059 [M18059]; decision D0060
\paragraph{Reliability of the extraction.} On the eight studies read by both extractors, the values (616 of 616) and metric directions (75 of 76) of the contrast units both found agree, as do 65 of 72 appraisal scores and 50 of 54 descriptive study-level flags (whether a study makes a comparison, reports a budget or variation); the three direction flags behind the verdicts agree in only 5 of 8 pairs, so the directions below rest on one extractor's reading. % direction flags: rq-results.json D0060 agreement_extra study_level_flags RQ2_better_majority 3/3, RQ3_anchor_better_majority 0/2, RQ4_sweep_up_majority 2/3 = 5/8; the other 11 flags 50/54
 The two extractors cut the same tables into comparison groups very differently: of the 245 groups they listed together, 36 were found by both (F1 0.29), and the factor type of a matched group agrees in 23 of 36. Unit totals are therefore not used; every statement below is about studies. % src: agreement-8-texts/agreement-stage-a.json (M-val 616/616 over the units both found, from 4 of the 8 studies; M-sign 75/76; F1_G numerator 72 = 2 x 36 matched, denominator 245; M-class 23/36); rq-results.json D0060 agreement_extra (QA item scores 65/72; study-level flags 55/62)
\paragraph{Verdicts.} The protocol names four verdicts without defining them. We define them as follows, after the results were known, and apply them mechanically to RQ2 to RQ5 on the included studies (Section~\ref{sec:method-deviations}): \emph{untested} if fewer than five sampled studies give a signed direction; \emph{mixed} if neither direction holds in at least two thirds of them (weighted); \emph{weakly supported} if one direction holds in at least two thirds but fewer than half of the studies making the comparison report both its budget and its run-to-run variation; \emph{well supported} if one direction holds in at least two thirds and at least half report both. RQ1 and RQ6 describe the evidence and are not graded. The verdicts are the same when the 27 records the validator still rejected are left out (except RQ5, which then has four signed studies and becomes untested) and when the second extractor's records replace the adjudicated ones (Supplement~S6). % src: decision D0060; rq-results.json D0060 verdicts (included, included_without_rejected, included_EB_on_8)

\paragraph{Formulations in the sample (RQ1).} Absorbing (masked) discrete state spaces are the most common: 27 of 59 included studies, 0.59 [0.45, 0.73] of the included studies; continuous latent spaces appear in 17, 13 of them unified models. The rule that decides which positions to commit is confidence-ranked in 9 studies and threshold-adaptive in 5, all positions are updated together in 22, and 9 do not state it. Blockwise (semi-autoregressive) generation is used in 12 (0.24 [0.12, 0.38]). Sixteen studies give the sampler no way to revise a committed token; 25 keep no memory across steps and 5 use an exact key--value cache. Eleven are training-free (0.24 [0.12, 0.38]): the contribution is a sampler, cache or guidance rule applied to an existing model. % src: rq-results.json D0060 included RQ1_state_space=discrete-absorbing 27/59 0.594 [0.45, 0.731]; continuous-latent 17 (G-mm 13 in RQ1.state_space by_stratum); position_rule confidence-ranked 9, threshold-adaptive 5, all-positions 22, not_stated 9; blockwise yes 12 0.243 [0.122, 0.377]; revision none 16; memory none 25, exact-cache 5; training_free yes 11 0.237 [0.12, 0.375]

\subsection{RQ2: diffusion against autoregressive models}
\label{sec:rq2}
Twenty-five of the 59 sampled included studies place a diffusion arm and an autoregressive arm in the same table (0.40 [0.26, 0.54] of the included studies). One of the 25 matches the compute budget of the two arms in at least one such comparison (0.06 [0.00, 0.20]) and none in all of them (upper bound 0.18); 23 leave the budget of one arm unreported in at least one (0.93 [0.79, 1.00]). Five state that they copied the autoregressive numbers from another paper rather than running the baseline (0.20 [0.03, 0.40]), and 3 report run-to-run variation for these comparisons (0.18 [0.00, 0.37]). % src: rq-results.json D0060 included RQ2_has_ar 25/59 0.396 [0.255, 0.537]; RQ2_matched_any 1/25 0.06 [0.0, 0.204]; RQ2_matched_all 0/25 [0, 0.18]; RQ2_budget_not_reported_any 23/25 0.928 [0.785, 1.0]; RQ2_copied_stated_any 5/25 0.198 [0.028, 0.397]; RQ2_variance_any 3/25 0.181 [0.0, 0.369]; group_counts_included ar_groups_by_budget {not reported 75, reported 23, matched 3}
The direction is split. Of the 22 studies with a signed contrast, diffusion is better on most of its units in 12 and worse in 8 (weighted 0.45 [0.21, 0.71] and 0.46 [0.21, 0.71]); the comparisons pit the best diffusion arm against the best autoregressive arm with no matching of size or budget, so the split says more about the baselines chosen than about the model families. Seven of the 59 studies claim in the abstract that their diffusion model matches, approaches or beats an autoregressive model (0.15 [0.05, 0.26]). Half of the seed studies make the comparison (10 of 20), and there diffusion is worse in 6 of the 10. % src: D0060 included RQ2_signed 22, RQ2_better_majority 12/22 0.452 [0.212, 0.711], RQ2_worse_majority 8/22 0.461 [0.212, 0.711]; RQ2_abstract_claim 7/59 0.145 [0.048, 0.26]; seed RQ2_has_ar 10/20, RQ2_worse_majority 6/10
\textbf{Verdict: mixed} (22 signed studies; neither direction reaches two thirds), and the comparison is almost never made at a stated common budget.

\subsection{RQ3: what training strategies contribute}
\label{sec:rq3}
Twenty-seven sampled included studies contrast training choices within the paper (0.44 [0.31, 0.58]). In 18 of the 24 with a signed contrast, the paper's anchor configuration (its focal or default setting, or its first own arm when neither is stated) is better on most units (0.79 [0.59, 0.95]); in 3 it is worse. % src: D0060 included RQ3_has_training 27/59 0.444 [0.308, 0.583]; RQ3_anchor_better_majority 18/24 0.789 [0.593, 0.951]; RQ3_anchor_worse_majority 3/24; group_counts_included rq3_anchor_rules_signed_groups {focal-label 32, stated-default 21, first-own-arm 42, value-identity 24, other-own-model 2}
The control behind these contrasts is thin. Three of the 27 state for every training contrast what was held fixed (0.11 [0.00, 0.26]), 7 ablate the very component they credit (0.28 [0.10, 0.49]), and 4 report run-to-run variation (0.17 [0.01, 0.35]); 3 report both a budget and variation (0.12). % src: D0060 included RQ3_held_fixed_all 3/27 0.113 [0.0, 0.255]; RQ3_credited_ablation_any 7/27 0.282 [0.099, 0.49]; RQ3_variance_any 4/27 0.171 [0.013, 0.351]; RQ3_control 3/27 0.118
\textbf{Verdict: weakly supported.} The direction favours the papers' anchor configurations (two thirds is passed), but few contrasts isolate the change and almost none show that the gain exceeds run-to-run variation.

\subsection{RQ4: quality against inference compute}
\label{sec:rq4}
Inference settings are varied in 31 of the 59 sampled included studies (0.65 [0.53, 0.78]), most often the number of steps or network calls (14), guidance (9) and the tokens committed per step (7). Twenty-four of the 31 report the inference budget for at least one such contrast (0.81 [0.66, 0.94]) and 8 report run-to-run variation (0.29 [0.14, 0.46]). % src: D0060 included RQ4_has_inference 31/59 0.651 [0.525, 0.775]; RQ4_budget_reported_any 24/31 0.806 [0.655, 0.939]; RQ4_variance_any 8/31 0.29 [0.135, 0.464]; RQ4.by_factor by_stratum, update strata summed: steps-or-nfe 14, guidance 9, tokens-per-step 7
Fifteen included studies sweep the number of steps or the tokens per step. Read along increasing compute, most of a study's curves improve in 9 of them (0.61 [0.34, 0.87]) and every curve improves in 5 (0.37 [0.12, 0.64]); 7 show at least one curve that peaks inside the range or moves both ways (0.47 [0.20, 0.72]), and ignoring changes under 1\% does not remove them (7 of 13). Two studies supply most of the curves, which is why we count studies. % src: D0060 included RQ4_has_sweep 15/59; RQ4_sweep_up_majority 9/15 0.609 [0.335, 0.866]; RQ4_sweep_all_up 5/15 0.37 [0.123, 0.643]; RQ4_any_nonmonotone 7/15 0.467 [0.2, 0.72]; RQ4_any_nonmonotone_tol1pct 7/13; group_counts_included sweep_curves_by_study (M15781 62, M17249 60 of 170)
\textbf{Verdict: mixed} (15 sweeping studies; improvement with compute holds in 0.61, short of two thirds). More compute often helps, but a fixed step count or parallelism level is not a safe default, and the census shows that even the value rule behind a headline number is often unstated (Section~\ref{sec:census}).

\subsection{RQ5: synergy in unified models}
\label{sec:rq5}
The unified stratum gives 20 sampled studies of 52. Three claim in the abstract that understanding and generation help each other; none of the 20 uses a benchmark built to test that claim (upper bound 0.17 for the 52). Eight contain an ablation that removes a task or modality or compares shared with decoupled parameters, and two of the three claiming studies are among them. Five of the eight give signed results; read as the joint (shared or fuller) model against the reduced (decoupled) one, the joint model is better on most units in 2 and worse in 3. % src: D0060 included RQ5_claim 3/20; RQ5_benchmark 0/20 [0, 0.168]; RQ5_has_ablation 8/20; RQ5 claim_and_group 2; RQ5_signed 5; RQ5_joint_better_majority 2/5, RQ5_joint_worse_majority 3/5 (orientation by arm: analysis/extraction/d0052/rq5-orientation.jsonl; group_counts_included rq5_signed_by_study)
The abstract claim types come from an earlier rubric in which the synergy claim type did not reach reliability, so the count of three is descriptive (Section~\ref{sec:method-deviations}).
\textbf{Verdict: mixed} (five signed studies, split 2 to 3), and untested on a purpose-built benchmark.

\subsection{RQ6: how rigorous the evidence is}
\label{sec:rq6}
Table~\ref{tab:qa-summary} gives the nine appraisal items for the included studies, with the seed column beside them; shares below are at score 2 on the low reading (an unclear sub-item counts as not met). Code is released for a sixth of the included studies (QA1: 9 of 59, 0.17 [0.07, 0.29]); of the 19 sampled studies, in either column, that say code is released, 17 print an own-code URL that resolved on 2026-10-21 and 2 print none. Weights and data behind the main comparison are both released in 1 study (QA2: 0.02 [0.00, 0.07]). The main comparison is repeated over runs in 10 of 54 studies (QA4: 0.20 [0.09, 0.33]) and given an interval, standard error or test in 9 (QA5: 0.22 [0.10, 0.35]). Budgets of both arms are reported in 0.40 [0.25, 0.55], the credited component is ablated in 0.26 [0.14, 0.41] (0.46 on the high reading), and limitations with a failure case are stated in 0.60 [0.46, 0.74]. The seed column differs most on code release (0.40) and ablation (0.67), and is lower on repeated runs and uncertainty (0.05 each). % src: tables/qa-summary.tex (scripts/qa_tables.py from rq-results.json D0060 included and seed); qa1-links.jsonl (19 rows: 17 yes, 2 no-url)
The claims in the papers mostly agree with their own tables. Of 179 claims listed for the included studies, 49 match a table number exactly, 55 match its direction, 32 match in part, 31 cannot be checked against the text and 12 are not supported by any printed number; none is contradicted by the paper's own table. Seven of 54 included studies make at least one unsupported claim (0.14 [0.04, 0.26]); none of the 20 seed studies does. Agreement between the two extractors on claim status is moderate ($\kappa = 0.44$ on 33 matched claims), so these counts are indicative. % src: rq-results.json D0060 group_counts_included claims_status {match 49, direction 55, partial 32, not-checkable 31, unsupported 12} (sum 179); D0060 included claims_any_unsupported 7/54 0.139 [0.043, 0.259], seed 0/20; agreement_extra claim_status kappa 0.4426, 33 pairs
