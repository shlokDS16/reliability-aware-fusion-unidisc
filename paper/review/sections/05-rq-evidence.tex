\section{What the evidence shows, by research question}
\label{sec:rq}

This section answers the six research questions from the 615 included studies. Two kinds of number appear. Counts over all included studies come from fields the full-text screeners recorded (Section~\ref{sec:method-extraction}). Statements about how studies ran their comparisons, and the quality appraisal, come from the stratified sample of 80 studies; they are given as the number of sampled studies and as a share weighted to the size of each stratum, with a 95\% interval. Every count describes the included studies, which lean towards recent work and leave out the seed corpus (Section~\ref{sec:limits}); none describes the field as a whole. % src: analysis/rq1/rq1-counts.json; analysis/extraction/d0052/sample.json; gate-manifest.json frame sizes

\subsection{RQ1: which formulations exist}
\label{sec:rq1}

Of the 615 included studies, 557 qualify through a diffusion model whose output includes text or code (criterion I1(a)), 41 through a unified model that understands and generates across text and another modality (I1(b)), and 17 through both. In 12 of the I1(a) studies no reported result is text: the model generates only non-language strings, and these studies are counted apart. % src: analysis/rq1/rq1-counts.json i1 {"a": 557, "b": 41, "ab": 17}, nonlang_only 12 (after the D0059 field resolutions)
Both screeners recorded the study's modality as text only for 410 studies and as involving image, video, audio, speech or 3D for 164; 28 involve other non-text inputs or outputs and 13 were recorded differently by the two screeners. % src: rq1-counts.json mm (text 410, multimodal 164, other 28; splits 3 + 2 + 2 + 6 = 13)
The included studies are recent: 449 are dated 2026 and 111 are dated 2025, against 55 for 2021 to 2024 together. Unified I1(b) or I1(a)-and-(b) studies number 36 in 2026, 12 in 2025 and 10 earlier. % src: rq1-counts.json year {2021: 3, 2022: 5, 2023: 13, 2024: 34, 2025: 111, 2026: 449}; i1_by_year (2026 b 25 + ab 11 = 36; 2025 b 8 + ab 4 = 12; earlier b 1 + ab 2 + b 7 = 10)
The screeners also marked which research questions each study carries evidence for, by pointing to a results table or figure. Both screeners marked an autoregressive baseline on the same task in the study's own results (RQ2) for 246 studies, a within-paper contrast of a training choice (RQ3) for 237, a within-paper contrast of an inference setting (RQ4) for 394, and, among unified studies, a synergy ablation, benchmark or claim (RQ5) for 32. % src: rq1-counts.json rq_both {"RQ2": 246, "RQ3": 237, "RQ4": 394, "RQ5": 32}; criteria-v1.6-screener.md "RQ TAGS"

The remaining statements rest on the extraction sample: 79 studies in the frame (19 long and 20 short language-only studies, 20 unified studies and 20 seed-corpus studies; one sampled study left the frame when its screening fields were resolved). Shares are weighted to the estimated stratum sizes, so a stratum of 20 that stands for 1,074 seed-corpus papers counts for more than a stratum of 20 that stands for 52 unified studies; the unweighted count is always given beside the share. % src: rq-results.json n_by_stratum {G-long 19, G-mm 20, G-short 20, S-seed 20}, N_by_stratum {G-mm 52, G-short 267, G-long 265, S-seed 1073.8}, left_frame_D0059 [M18059]
Two cautions apply to every count of comparisons below. The extraction records a paper's comparisons as groups of arms with contrast units; on the eight studies read by both extractors, values and directions of the units both extractors found agree almost always, but the two extractors split the same tables into groups differently, so unit totals are indicative and study-level statements are the ones we draw conclusions from (Section~\ref{sec:method-extraction}). And a verdict summarises the direction of the evidence together with its control: \emph{well supported} when the comparisons point the same way and hold budgets and report variation, \emph{mixed} when directions split, \emph{weakly supported} when the direction is consistent but the comparisons are rarely controlled, and \emph{untested} when almost no study makes the comparison. % src: analysis/extraction/d0052/agreement-8-texts/agreement-stage-a.json (G4 M-val 616/616, M-sign 75/76; G4 F1_G 72/245, F1_K 300/1227); protocol.md section 7 names the four verdicts but does not define them; the definitions here are ours (listed among the departures)
No verdict changes when the estimates are recomputed without the seed stratum, without the 27 records the validator still rejected, or with the second extractor's records for the eight double-read studies (Supplement~S6). The largest moves come from the seed stratum: without it, the share of studies with an inference contrast that report its budget rises from 0.55 to 0.81, and the share stating what was held fixed in every training contrast falls from 0.33 to 0.11. % src: rq-results.json sensitivity: RQ4_budget_reported_any S0 0.551, S1 0.806; RQ3_held_fixed_stated_all S0 0.327, S1 0.113; S2 and S3 columns

\paragraph{Formulations in the sample.} Absorbing (masked) discrete state spaces dominate: 42 of 79 sampled studies, an estimated 0.70 of the frame [0.55, 0.81]; continuous latent spaces appear in 18 studies, 13 of them unified models, and uniform or flow-based discrete spaces in 6. The position rule that decides which tokens to commit is confidence-ranked in 15 studies and threshold-adaptive in 10 (together about 0.46 of the frame), and 12 studies do not state it. Blockwise (semi-autoregressive) generation is used in 17 studies (0.25 [0.14, 0.40]). Most studies give the sampler no way to revise a committed token (26 none, 28 only through an implicit channel such as a re-noised context), and 37 keep no memory across steps; exact or approximate key--value caches appear in 9. A third of the frame is training-free (18 studies; 0.31 [0.19, 0.47]): the contribution is a sampler, cache or guidance rule applied to an existing model. % src: rq-results.json RQ1.state_space (discrete-absorbing 42/79 0.695 [0.55, 0.809]; continuous-latent 18, G-mm 13/20; discrete-uniform 4 + discrete-flow 2), position_rule (confidence-ranked 15 0.261; threshold-adaptive 10 0.203; not_stated 12), blockwise yes 17 0.247 [0.141, 0.397], revision (none 26; implicit-channel 28), memory (none 37; exact-cache 6 + approximate-cache 3), training_free yes 18 0.31 [0.188, 0.466]

\subsection{RQ2: diffusion against autoregressive models}
\label{sec:rq2}
Thirty-five of the 79 sampled studies place a diffusion arm and an autoregressive arm in the same table (an estimated 0.46 of the frame [0.32, 0.61]). None of the 35 matches the compute budget across the two arms in every such comparison (0 of 35; upper bound 0.10), and in 31 at least one diffusion--autoregressive comparison reports no budget for one of the arms (0.84 [0.59, 0.95]). Seven take the autoregressive numbers from another paper rather than running the baseline (0.20 [0.08, 0.43]), and three report any run-to-run variation for these comparisons. % src: rq-results.json RQ2 has_ar_group 35/79 0.463 [0.321, 0.612]; ar_budget_matched_all 0/35 [0, 0.099]; ar_budget_not_reported_any 31/35 0.839 [0.59, 0.949]; ar_numbers_copied_any 7/35 0.2 [0.075, 0.434]; ar_variance_any 3/35
The direction is split. Among the 32 studies with at least one signed contrast, diffusion is better on most of its units in 15 and worse in 14; over all units the count is 131 better, 129 worse and 2 ties. Unified studies lean towards diffusion (7 of 10 better), the seed corpus against it (6 of 10 worse). Ten of the 79 studies claim in the abstract that their diffusion model matches or beats an autoregressive model (0.15 [0.07, 0.29]). % src: RQ2 diffusion_better_majority_of_units 15/32 (G-mm 7/10), diffusion_worse_majority_of_units 14/32 (S-seed 6/10); units_total {better 131, worse 129, tie 2}; abstract_claims_vs_ar_CT6 10/79 0.148 [0.07, 0.287]
\textbf{Verdict: mixed, and untested at matched budget.} The evidence does not show that diffusion language models are better or worse than autoregressive models; it shows that the comparison is almost never made at a stated common budget, so neither reading is supported.

\subsection{RQ3: what training strategies contribute}
\label{sec:rq3}
Thirty-three sampled studies contrast training choices within the paper (0.35 [0.23, 0.50]): the objective in 22, reinforcement-learning post-training in 7, the pretraining recipe and supervised fine-tuning in 6 each, distillation in 3 and initialisation from an autoregressive model in 1. The proposed recipe is better on most of its units in 20 of the 27 studies with signed contrasts (0.74 [0.42, 0.92]; 289 units better, 113 worse, 16 ties). % src: rq-results.json RQ3 has_training_group 33/79 0.351 [0.227, 0.498]; by_factor objective 22, rl 7, pretraining-recipe 6, sft 6, distillation 3, ar-initialisation 1; anchor_better_majority 20/27 0.739 [0.417, 0.918]; units_total {anchor-better 289, anchor-worse 113, tie 16}
The control behind these contrasts is thin. Only 6 of the 33 state for every training contrast what was held fixed (0.33 [0.14, 0.59]), 9 ablate the very component they credit (0.31 [0.13, 0.57]), and 4 report variation over runs or seeds. % src: RQ3 held_fixed_stated_all 6/33 0.327 [0.142, 0.588]; credited_ablation_any 9/33 0.31 [0.133, 0.569]; variance_any 4/33
\textbf{Verdict: weakly supported.} The direction favours the recipes the papers propose, as one would expect from papers that report their own method, but few contrasts isolate the recipe and almost none show that the gain exceeds run-to-run variation.

\subsection{RQ4: quality against inference compute}
\label{sec:rq4}
Inference settings are the most often varied factor: 47 of 79 studies contrast at least one (0.75 [0.61, 0.85]), most often the number of steps or network calls (18), guidance (13), tokens committed per step (11), the order rule (10) and block or parallel decoding (10); caching appears in 7 and remasking in 5. Thirty-one of the 47 report the inference budget for at least one such contrast (0.55 [0.38, 0.71]) and 12 report variation (0.26 [0.14, 0.44]). % src: rq-results.json RQ4 has_inference_group 47/79 0.748 [0.609, 0.849]; by_factor steps-or-nfe 18, guidance 13, tokens-per-step 11, order-rule 10, block-or-parallel 10, cache 7, remasking 5; budget_reported_any 31/47 0.551 [0.377, 0.712]; variance_any 12/47 0.262 [0.138, 0.441]
Read along increasing compute (more steps, or fewer tokens per step), 139 of 181 sweep curves improve throughout, 12 worsen throughout, 16 peak inside the range and 14 move both ways. A non-monotone curve is not rare at the level of studies: 10 of the 19 studies with a sweep show at least one (0.62 [0.34, 0.84]). % src: RQ4 dose_shapes_units {up 139, down 12, peaked 16, other 14} (sum 181); dose_shapes_by_factor; any_nonmonotone_dose_curve 10/19 0.62 [0.34, 0.837]
\textbf{Verdict: well supported for the direction, weakly supported for its size.} More compute usually buys quality, but a fixed step count or parallelism level is not a safe default: in about half the studies with a sweep, quality stops improving or falls inside the tested range, and the census shows that the setting behind a headline number is often not stated (Section~\ref{sec:census}).

\subsection{RQ5: synergy in unified models}
\label{sec:rq5}
The unified stratum gives 20 studies. Three claim in the abstract that understanding and generation help each other; none uses a benchmark built to test that claim. Eight of the 20 contain an ablation that removes a task or modality or compares shared with decoupled parameters; two of the three claiming studies have such an ablation and one does not. Five of the eight ablating studies give signed units: in three the paper's full (joint) model is better on most units and in two it is worse. % src: rq-results.json RQ5 n_mm_extracted 20; CT5_claim {yes 3, no 17}; synergy_benchmark {no 20}; synergy_group {True 8, False 12}; claim_and_group 2; claim_without_group [R0963]; group_ids signed: M18816 Table 31 (+,+,+,+ / +,+,-,+), M15992 Table S7 (+ x5), M13884 Table 23 (+ x3), M19293 Table 7 (- x5, 0), M03633 Table 5 (-); unsigned: M09294, M19012, M15792
\textbf{Verdict: untested.} Synergy is claimed in few unified studies and measured on no purpose-built benchmark; the ablations that bear on it point both ways, and no sampled study measures it on a benchmark designed for it.

\subsection{RQ6: how rigorous the evidence is}
\label{sec:rq6}
Table~\ref{tab:qa-summary} gives the nine appraisal items. Reproducibility is better than the statistics. Code is released and reachable for a third of the frame (QA1 at score 2: 17 studies, 0.32 [0.20, 0.48]; on the dated check of 2026-10-21, 17 of the 19 code links the extractors found resolved). Weights and data for the main comparison are fully released far less often (QA2 at 2: 4 studies, 0.11 [0.04, 0.25]). The main comparison is repeated over runs in 11 of 74 studies (QA4 at 2: 0.10 [0.05, 0.20]) and given an interval, standard error or test in 10 (QA5 at 2: 0.10 [0.05, 0.20]). Budgets of both arms are reported in half of the frame (QA6), the credited component is ablated in about half (QA7), and limitations with a failure case are stated in most (QA9 at 2: 0.70 [0.55, 0.81]). % src: tables/qa-summary.tex (generated by scripts/qa_tables.py from rq-results.json RQ6); qa1-links.jsonl (17 of 19 resolve, checked 2026-10-21)
The claims in the papers agree with their own tables more often than not. Of 260 claims the extractors listed, 64 match a table number exactly and 84 match its direction, 49 match in part, 51 cannot be checked against the text, and 12 are not supported by any printed number; no claim is contradicted by the paper's own table. Seven of 74 studies make at least one unsupported claim (0.05 [0.01, 0.14]; 7 of 54 in the update strata). % src: RQ6 claims_status {match 64, direction 84, partial 49, not-checkable 51, unsupported 12} (sum 260, in-frame records); claims_vs_tables_QA8 any_contradicted_or_unsupported 7/74 0.046 [0.014, 0.143]; update_strata 7/54 0.139
\textbf{Verdict: the evidence base is reproducible in part and statistically weak.} The appraisal for each sampled study is in Supplement~S6.
