\subsection{OP1. What a step sweep identifies}
\label{sec:open-op1}

A step sweep identifies the learning-term difference between a position rule and its base only at one token per step (Section~\ref{sec:budget-props}). The statement is derived, with a toy enumeration in which the learning-term gain at two tokens per step departed from the one-token gain by more than 25\% or in sign in 11 of 12 cases (E0). % src: framework-v2.md section C (P1); red-team M6 (audits/2026-10-14-red-team-selection-term.md)
The census counts how often a sweep is reported with the value rule said to be fixed: 55 of 152 papers with a generated-sample metric report the headline at two or more step settings, and 20 of those state that the value rule was held fixed (Section~\ref{sec:census-results}). % src: coverage/census-result-v2.md; Table~\ref{tab:census-main}
In the 47-model pilot table, one of the 14 models that use guidance (Show-o2) reports a step sweep with guidance held fixed. % src: design-space-v1.md section 5 (c) (Show-o2 Table 11); pilot, parts read only
Settling it needs a rerun of two position rules, for example random and confidence-ranked, on one frozen model, in which each rule's learning term is evaluated on the states visited by the other. If the learning-term difference at larger $k$ matched the one at $k=1$ within noise across rules and models, a sweep would identify more than we state.

\subsection{OP2. A revision sampler's gain against its value rule}
\label{sec:open-op2}

The question is whether a revision sampler's gain over its base at matched forward passes survives matching the value rule on both arms. We make no claim about any particular sampler. The closest published table (ReMDM, Section~\ref{sec:budget-props}) shows the gain but does not separate it from the effect of nucleus sampling (E2). % src: framework-v2.md section C (P3); primer-samplers.md section 2.7
The test is the revision sampler and its base at the same number of forward passes, with top-$p$ at 0.9 on both arms and with truncation off on both arms. Either outcome is informative. The claim that part of a reported revision gain belongs to the value rule would be refuted by a gain that persists with the rule matched.

\subsection{OP3. Cross-modal dependence in unified models}
\label{sec:open-op3}

The problem has two parts. P5a: the model's directed cross-modal dependence $S_\theta$ between co-committed text and image tokens is concentrated in early steps and on layout and object tokens. P5b: at matched forward passes and tokens per pass, a schedule that does not co-commit high-$S_\theta$ pairs scores better on text--image consistency than joint commitment, and a modality-sequential schedule need not beat an alternating one. Both concern the model, not the data law. % src: framework-v2.md section C (P5, P5a, P5b)
In the works we read, none measures a cross-modal quantity per step (E2, not exhaustive). Indirect hints exist. In UniDisc the distance between conditional and unconditional logits shrinks as tokens are decoded, and guidance on steps 1 to 3 of 24 gives CLIP 0.301, against 0.312 for all steps and 0.283 for steps 22 to 24~\cite{arXiv:2503.20853}. The one joint-against-sequential contrast we found, in an appendix ablation of MMaDA-Parallel (output alignment 59.8 parallel, 57.5 semi-parallel, 54.1 sequential; Appendix I, Table 10), is confounded: the sequential text cannot see the image and training differs~\cite{arXiv:2511.09611}; on our reading of its released code, the sampler also commits no image tokens in the first quarter of the steps, unlike the paper's text (an unverified code reading). % src: framework-v1.md section 4 (P5a, P5b evidence); framework-v2.md section C (last two lines); primer-multimodal.md section 4
The estimator, described in the supplement (Section~S8.5), draws the text set from the model's joint and compares the re-queried image marginals with their mean. Its algebra was checked on toy models only and says nothing about re-query fidelity on a real network.
A preregistered measurement on the same frozen model, reported in Section~\ref{sec:exp003}, concerns the sampler's role in joint text--image consistency and not the estimator above. % src: research/preregistration/EXP-003-value-rule-consistency.md header; gate audit 2026-10-15-gate-EXP-003.md; decisions D0019, D0025
An earlier preregistered attempt at P5a on the same frozen model stopped at its feasibility gate: under plain sampling at temperature 1 without guidance, the completion-specific text--image link measured by its gate statistic stayed below the preregistered threshold, so a per-step estimator had too little dependence to locate. P5a therefore needs a regime in which the joint link is clearly present before its timing can be measured. % src: decision D0016; runs/EXP-001/gates/verdict-pgv-rung1.json, verdict-pgv-rung2.json; reconciled with EXP-003 (decision D0025 item 5)

\subsection{OP4. A four-arm value-rule measurement on one checkpoint}
\label{sec:open-op4}

On one frozen checkpoint: sample-then-filter against its sample-select-resample twin, a confidence-ranked rank-then-sample sampler and the tilted twin, at matched forward passes and with an entropy-matched frontier. The contrast between the first two isolates choosing the set after seeing the values; the contrast between the twin and the confidence-ranked sampler isolates the position law; the tilted twin is the state-only sampler whose law matches sample-then-filter at one token per step. The red-team enumeration names this measurement as missing from the literature. % src: framework-v2.md section D item 4; method/selection-term.md T1.4 (the matched twin as reference)
The first check was how much of it the closest paper already covers. We read its text and code at the commit in its repository (E3)~\cite{arXiv:2605.12522}. It runs sample-then-filter at one token per step on small models trained from scratch, at three block lengths, and the resample twin. It does not run a confidence-ranked sampler that ranks by the largest marginal (its nearest arm ranks by entropy), a tilted twin, any temperature sweep or entropy-matched frontier, any step above one token, or any image or unified model, and its main figure has one seed and no intervals. % src: dossier/prior-art-2605.12522-experiments.md claims block (E3 at 8bafc9e; confidence 0.85 to 0.95)
The work left is the four arms with the frontier, at more than one token per step, on a text model and preferably a unified one. If sample-then-filter and its resampled twin agreed at matched passes and matched entropy, choosing after seeing the values would not be what moves the numbers. % src: design-space-v1.md claims block (next_test, falsifier)

\subsection{Reporting}
\label{sec:open-reporting}

The census (Section~\ref{sec:census}) is the evidence for a reporting problem, and it gives a checklist its content: the value rule of the headline result; the position rule; the guidance level and whether it was held fixed in any sweep; a quality--diversity frontier in place of a single point; and intervals or seeds. What is missing is mostly the first item: 50 of the 153 papers with a codable value rule do not state it. % src: coverage/census-result-v2.md (Result table); Section~\ref{sec:census-results}
