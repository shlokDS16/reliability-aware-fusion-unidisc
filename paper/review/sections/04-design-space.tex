\section{The design space, tested on 47 models}
\label{sec:design}

This section answers RQ1 for 47 models; the counts for all included studies are in Section~\ref{sec:rq}. The question is whether the vocabulary of Section~\ref{sec:notation} is enough to write down how each model generates, and where it is not.

\subsection{The test: fitting each model to the notation}
\label{sec:design-test}

For each model we filled the cells of C1 to C5 and assigned it to fourteen \emph{lever} rows, L1 to L14, each a design feature that changes quality or cost (Section~\ref{sec:budget-levers}), using only cell values the notation already names. A model \emph{fits fully} if every choice can be written with those values and the factorisation identity of Section~\ref{sec:budget} applies as it stands. It \emph{fits partly} if a cell needs a new value or the identity does not apply as written, and the missing piece is recorded. It does not fit if it cannot be placed. % src: coverage/design-space-v1.md section 0 ("fits")
The numbering of the levers and the assignment of each model to a lever are our reading of the mechanism the paper describes, not statements by the paper. % src: design-space-v1.md section 0 and the claims block header ("my numbering ... my inference")

The 47 models are a purposive sample, not a random one. The first 29 rows were copied into the notation from four earlier readings of primary sources (absorbing channels, sampler theory, Gaussian and embedding channels, unified multimodal models) and were not re-read for the table. The other 18 rows were read at section level: the method and sampling sections, the set-up paragraphs that state steps, temperature, truncation and guidance, and the step-sweep tables or paragraphs. Results tables other than step sweeps, proofs, most appendices and released code were not read, so no row rests on more than a reading of the paper, and none rests on code. % src: design-space-v1.md front matter ("coverage"), CSV column "evidence level" (29 "as recorded in the primer", 18 "for the sections listed"); counts in tables/design-space-counts.log
A statement ``not stated'' in the table means not found in the parts read.
By family, the 47 rows are 15 unified multimodal models and hybrids (32\%), 7 Gaussian, embedding or logit-simplex models, 6 absorbing, 5 sampler-only, 3 general discrete, 3 uniform and 4 adapted from autoregressive models (one with an edit channel), and one each for block, hybrid-channel, flow and distillation. % src: tables/design-space-counts.log ("groups"; the Gaussian group there counts CANDI, which Table 4 files under hybrid-channel); design-space-v1.md section 2
The multimodal share is higher than a balanced draw would give, because an existing reading of unified multimodal models supplied ten of the rows without further reading. % src: design-space-v1.md section 2 (last sentence)
Four of the 47 (Show-o, Dream~7B, UDLM and D-DiT) are not records of the curated list used to seed the corpus (Section~\ref{sec:method-sources}), which bears on the recall of that list and not on the table. % src: design-space-v1.md section 2 ("not in the corpus"; Show-o absent from the corpus)
Four candidates were left out, either because they repeated a row already present or because they had not been read (LLaDA~1.5, Dream-Coder~7B, LLaDA-V, Esoteric Language Models). % src: design-space-v1.md section 2 ("Dropped, and why")

\subsection{What fits fully, what fits partly, and the limits of the notation}
\label{sec:design-limits}

Of the 47 models, 36 fit the notation fully and 11 fit it partly; every model could be placed. % src: design-space-v1.csv column "fits (yes/partly/no)" (36 yes, 11 partly, 0 no; tables/design-space-counts.log); design-space-v1.md claims block (level E2, confidence 0.6)
We give no interval for this proportion, because the rows were not drawn at random and the count says nothing about the field. The 11 partial fits mark the limits of the notation. The Supplement (Section~S5) lists each with its provenance (taken from an earlier reading or read at section level), its evidence level and the extension it needs, and the released data give the full 47-row table. Six of the 11 come from five features that the notation cannot express without an extension, and five from two further reasons. Each is described below with the smallest extension we can see and what we recommend. % src: framework-v2.md section D1 (the five features); design-space-v1.md section 3(a) items 1 to 5 and 9

\paragraph{Five features beyond the notation (six rows).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{An edit channel that changes length} (Seed Diffusion, second stage~\cite{arXiv:2508.02193}). Corruption by insertion, deletion and substitution leaves no position index to which a per-position state can be attached, so the factorisation identity is undefined. We mark such channels as outside the identity of Section~\ref{sec:budget} and keep them in the taxonomy under token-level revision. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 1 (Seed Diffusion Sec 3.1, Eq 3-4, Remark 3.1)
  \item \emph{Stochastic memory} (Simplax~\cite{arXiv:2608.10615}). The state carries an auxiliary Dirichlet variable beside the categorical one. The identity then holds on the augmented path, and it is an equality for $x$ only if the memory $m_n$ is a deterministic function of the revealed path; in Simplax $m_n$ is a latent chain with its own kernel (in the variant used for the main results it is a function of the state and the prediction). We have not checked the augmented statement beyond this reading. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 2; framework-v1.md section 2.1 ("Attribution", tagged [I], open item)
  \item \emph{Graded evidence at uncommitted positions} (CANDI~\cite{arXiv:2510.22510}; related to the mixture channel of discrete flow matching~\cite{arXiv:2407.15595}). A position is either a clean anchor or a Gaussian-noised one-hot vector that evolves by an ordinary differential equation until it flips. The identity covers the flips conditional on the continuous residual; the continuous part needs the per-step term of Section~\ref{sec:budget-other}. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 3
  \item \emph{Predictors that output a joint over a set} (Di4C~\cite{arXiv:2410.08709}, Discrete Copula Diffusion~\cite{arXiv:2410.01949}). C3 assumes marginals; here the head returns a latent-mixture joint, or a copula combines two models. The joint draw (C4) is then a property of the predictor. The extension needed is a ``joint predictor over any subset''. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 4
  \item \emph{Sequence-level selection} (DiffuSeq, minimum Bayes risk over ten samples~\cite{arXiv:2210.08933}; also the optional judge-based best-of-$n$ of FUDOKI~\cite{arXiv:2505.20147}). Choosing among finished samples is neither revision nor a token-level value rule. The lever table has no row for it, and Section~\ref{sec:budget-mechanisms} places it outside the commit-kernel form. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 5
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Two further reasons (five rows).} The continuous image channels of Transfusion, BAGEL, Show-o2 and D-DiT~\cite{arXiv:2408.11039,arXiv:2505.14683,arXiv:2506.15564,arXiv:2501.00289} are placed in the notation but have no committed set, so the cross-modal part of the factorisation term is undefined for them (Section~\ref{sec:budget-crossmodal}). FUDOKI uses a metric-structured kernel, a softmax of embedding distance, that is none of the listed channels; it falls under the general transition matrices of D3PM~\cite{arXiv:2107.03006} and we place it with the symmetric channels. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 9 and "Not misfits" (continuous channels are placement only); CSV rows M24, M26 to M28, M46

\paragraph{Models that fit fully but still needed refinements.} Four refinements were needed for models that fit fully; they are listed in the Supplement (Section~S8.3).
Three features that look like misfits are not: the shift operation of Dream~7B, DiffuLLaMA and TESS~2 only relabels the marginals, complementary masks change the estimator but not the marginal reveal law, and the token-level reweighting of Dream~7B is an objective weight. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) "Not misfits"

\subsection{What the test could and could not fail}
\label{sec:design-weak}
The fit count is weak evidence, for four reasons: the vocabulary was written to be inclusive; the test is partly in-sample (25 of the first 29 rows fit fully, against 11 of the 18 read after the notation was fixed); 29 rows were taken over from earlier readings by a single reader; and the more informative results are the cells that could not be filled. No model contradicted the vocabulary, and seven of the 18 later rows needed a stated extension; this is not evidence that the vocabulary is correct for the field. The four reasons in full, and the comparison with the taxonomies of the two closest surveys, are in the Supplement (Section~S5.4). % src: design-space-v1.md competing_explanations, section 3; moved text in supplement.tex S5.3

\subsection{Fit of the unified multimodal models}
\label{sec:design-unified}

Fifteen of the 47 rows are unified models, and for them the notation needs four additions: guidance (including whole-modality dropout), per-modality loss weights and schedules, continuous image channels in the hybrids, and output-level revision by planning or reflection loops. The factorisation term gains a cross-modal part only for tokens of two discrete modalities that are committed in the same step (Section~\ref{sec:budget-crossmodal}). % src: framework-v1.md section 3 (last paragraph, "four additions, not no new machinery"); design-space-v1.csv family counts
