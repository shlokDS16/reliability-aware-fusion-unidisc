\section{The design space, tested on 47 models}
\label{sec:design}

This section answers RQ1 for 47 models. It does not yet answer RQ1 for every included study (Section~\ref{sec:intro-rq}). The question is whether the vocabulary of Section~\ref{sec:notation} is enough to write down how each model generates, and where it is not.

\subsection{The test}
\label{sec:design-test}

For each model we filled the cells of C1 to C5 and assigned it to fourteen \emph{lever} rows, L1 to L14 (Section~\ref{sec:budget-levers}), using only cell values the notation already names. A model \emph{fits} if every choice can be written with those values and the factorisation identity of Section~\ref{sec:budget} applies as it stands. It fits \emph{partly} if a cell needs a new value or the identity does not apply as written, and the missing piece is recorded. It does not fit if it cannot be placed. % src: coverage/design-space-v1.md section 0 ("fits")
The lever rows are our numbering of the rows of the lever table, and the assignment of a model to a lever is our reading of the mechanism it describes, not a statement by the paper. % src: design-space-v1.md section 0 and the claims block header ("my numbering ... my inference")

The 47 models are a purposive sample, not a random one. Twenty-nine rows (M01 to M29) were copied into the notation from four earlier readings of primary sources (absorbing channels, sampler theory, Gaussian and embedding channels, unified multimodal models) and were not re-read for the table. Eighteen rows (M30 to M47) were read at section level: the method and sampling sections, the set-up paragraphs that state steps, temperature, truncation and guidance, and the step-sweep tables or paragraphs. Results tables other than step sweeps, proofs, most appendices and released code were not read, so no row is above evidence level E2 and none rests on code (E3). % src: design-space-v1.md front matter ("coverage"), CSV column "evidence level" (29 "as recorded in the primer", 18 "for the sections listed"); counts in tables/design-space-counts.log
A statement ``not stated'' in the table means not found in the parts read.
By family the rows are: unified multimodal models and hybrids 15 (32\%), Gaussian, embedding or logit-simplex models 7, absorbing 6, sampler-only 5, general discrete 3, uniform 3, adapted from autoregressive models 4 (one with an edit channel), and one each for block, hybrid-channel, flow and distillation. % src: tables/design-space-counts.log ("groups"; the Gaussian group there counts CANDI, which Table 4 files under hybrid-channel); design-space-v1.md section 2
The multimodal share is higher than a balanced draw would give, because the multimodal reading supplied ten rows at no extra reading cost. % src: design-space-v1.md section 2 (last sentence)
Four of the 47 (Show-o, Dream~7B, UDLM and D-DiT) are not records of the curated list that seeds our corpus, which bears on the recall of that list and not on the table. % src: design-space-v1.md section 2 ("not in the corpus"; Show-o absent from the corpus)
Four candidates were dropped for repeating a row already present or for not being read (LLaDA~1.5, Dream-Coder~7B, LLaDA-V, Esoteric Language Models). % src: design-space-v1.md section 2 ("Dropped, and why")

\subsection{Result: what fits, and the stated limits}
\label{sec:design-limits}

Thirty-six of the 47 models fit, 11 fit partly, and none was left unplaced. % src: design-space-v1.csv column "fits (yes/partly/no)" (36 yes, 11 partly, 0 no; tables/design-space-counts.log); design-space-v1.md claims block (level E2, confidence 0.6)
We give no interval for this proportion, because the rows were not drawn at random and the count says nothing about the field. The 11 partial fits are the stated limits of the notation. Six of them come from five features that the notation does not express without an extension, and five from two further reasons. Each is stated with the smallest extension we can see, and with what we recommend. % src: framework-v2.md section D1 (the five features); design-space-v1.md section 3(a) items 1 to 5 and 9

\paragraph{Five features beyond the notation (six rows).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{An edit channel that changes length} (Seed Diffusion, second stage~\cite{arXiv:2508.02193}). Corruption by insertion, deletion and substitution leaves no position index to which a per-position state can be attached, so the committed-set identity is undefined. We mark such channels as outside the identity of Section~\ref{sec:budget} and keep them in the taxonomy under token-level revision. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 1 (Seed Diffusion Sec 3.1, Eq 3-4, Remark 3.1)
  \item \emph{Stochastic memory} (Simplax~\cite{arXiv:2608.10615}). The state carries an auxiliary Dirichlet variable beside the categorical one. The identity then holds on the augmented path and is an equality for $x$ only if $m_n$ is a deterministic function of the revealed path; here $m_n$ is a latent chain with its own kernel (in the variant used for the main results it is a function of the state and the prediction). We have not checked the augmented statement beyond this reading. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 2; framework-v1.md section 2.1 ("Attribution", tagged [I], open item)
  \item \emph{Graded evidence at uncommitted positions} (CANDI~\cite{arXiv:2510.22510}; related to the mixture channel of discrete flow matching~\cite{arXiv:2407.15595}). A position is either a clean anchor or a Gaussian-noised one-hot vector that evolves by an ordinary differential equation until it flips. The identity covers the flips conditional on the continuous residual; the continuous part needs the per-step term of Section~\ref{sec:budget-other}. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 3
  \item \emph{Predictors that output a joint over a set} (Di4C~\cite{arXiv:2410.08709}, Discrete Copula Diffusion~\cite{arXiv:2410.01949}). C3 assumes marginals; here the head returns a latent-mixture joint, or a copula combines two models. The joint draw (C4) is then a property of the predictor. The extension is ``joint predictor over any subset''. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 4
  \item \emph{Sequence-level selection} (DiffuSeq, minimum Bayes risk over ten samples~\cite{arXiv:2210.08933}; also the optional judge-based best-of-$n$ of FUDOKI~\cite{arXiv:2505.20147}). Choosing among finished samples is neither revision nor a token-level value rule. The lever table has no row for it, and Section~\ref{sec:budget-mechanisms} places it outside the commit-kernel form. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 5
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Two further reasons (five rows).} The continuous image channels of Transfusion, BAGEL, Show-o2 and D-DiT~\cite{arXiv:2408.11039,arXiv:2505.14683,arXiv:2506.15564,arXiv:2501.00289} are placed in the notation but have no committed set, so the cross-modal part of the factorisation term is undefined for them (Section~\ref{sec:budget-crossmodal}). FUDOKI uses a metric-structured kernel, a softmax of embedding distance, that is none of the listed channels; it falls under the general transition matrices of D3PM~\cite{arXiv:2107.03006} and we place it with the symmetric channels. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) item 9 and "Not misfits" (continuous channels are placement only); CSV rows M24, M26 to M28, M46

\paragraph{Rows marked ``yes'' that still changed the wording.} Four refinements were needed for rows that fit; they are listed in the supplement (Section~S8.2).
Three features that look like misfits are not: the shift operation of Dream~7B, DiffuLLaMA and TESS~2 only relabels the marginals, complementary masks change the estimator but not the marginal reveal law, and the token-level reweighting of Dream~7B is an objective weight. % src: design-space-v1.md section 3(a) "Not misfits"

\subsection{What the test could and could not fail}
\label{sec:design-weak}
The fit count is weak evidence: the vocabulary was written to be inclusive, the test is partly in-sample (25 of the first 29 rows fit, against 11 of the 18 read after the notation was fixed), 29 rows were copied from earlier readings by one model reader, and the sharper results are the cells that could not be filled. The result reads as ``the vocabulary was not contradicted by any of the 47 models, and seven of the 18 later rows needed a stated extension'', not as evidence that it is correct for the field. The four reasons in full, and the comparison with the taxonomies of the two closest surveys, are in the supplement (Section~S5.3). % src: design-space-v1.md competing_explanations, section 3; moved text in supplement.tex S5.3

\subsection{Unified multimodal models}
\label{sec:design-unified}

Fifteen of the 47 rows are unified models, and for them the notation needs four additions, not none: guidance (including whole-modality dropout), per-modality loss weights and schedules, continuous image channels in the hybrids, and output-level revision by planning or reflection loops. The factorisation term gains a cross-modal part only for co-committed tokens of two discrete modalities (Section~\ref{sec:budget-crossmodal}). % src: framework-v1.md section 3 (last paragraph, "four additions, not no new machinery"); design-space-v1.csv family counts
