\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}
\subsection{Model and Tokens}\label{sec:setting-model}
% run: runs/P2-UNIDISC-KEYCHECK/keycheck.json:{model_n_param_elements (= 1406870785), config.image_vocab_size (= 16384)}
The model is UniDisc~\cite{swerdlow2025unidisc}, a unified masked discrete diffusion model with 1.4B parameters, used as released with
no training or finetuning here.
A pair is a caption of $\ell$ text tokens and an image of $m$ image tokens in one sequence.
% run: SAP card v4 / protocol (design values: 512 px, 32 x 32 grid, captions of 5 to 32 tokens); keycheck.json:config.image_vocab_size
Images are tokenised at 512~px by the VQ-16 tokeniser of LlamaGen~\cite{sun2024llamagen} into a $32\times32$ grid, so
$m=1\,024$ tokens, each one of 16\,384 codes; captions have 5 to 32 text tokens.
The network $p_\theta$ maps a partly masked sequence to a categorical distribution at every masked position.
The backbone runs in \texttt{bf16}, and every log-probability is a 32-bit log-softmax of the logits restricted to the vocabulary of the
scored modality.
UniDisc's denoiser has no time input in the released configuration: time conditioning is off, the official inference configuration
passes a null noise level, the backbone builds no timestep embedder without time conditioning, and the released checkpoint contains no
time-embedding weights. The scorer uses the authors' null-sigma inference path.
This is a property of the released configuration and checkpoint, read in the authors' code at a pinned commit, and not a statement about
UniDisc in general (evidence and limits in supplement S-I).

\subsection{Per-Token Evidence and the Unweighted Decision}\label{sec:setting-evidence}
Write $\tilde v=(\tilde v_1,\dots,\tilde v_m)$ for the observed, possibly damaged image tokens, $c^+$ for the true caption, and $c^-$ for a
wrong caption.
For a caption $c$, let $\Lambda^{c}_j[y]$ be the log-probability of code $y$ at image position $j$ in one pass of $p_\theta$ with every
image token masked and $c$ visible.
The \emph{image-direction evidence} of token $j$ is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_j=\Lambda^{c^+}_j[\tilde v_j]-\Lambda^{c^-}_j[\tilde v_j],
\label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
the log-likelihood ratio of the observed code under the two captions; $\Delta_j>0$ when the observed code is more probable under the true caption.
Because every image token is masked in these passes, their logits do not depend on the image, which enters only through the codes at
which the log-probabilities are read.
The verification statistic is the unweighted sum, and the decision chooses the true caption when the sum is positive:
\begin{equation}
S=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Delta_j,\qquad Y=\ind\big[S>0\big],
\label{eq:sum}
\end{equation}
with a tie counted as wrong.
% F5 (professor triage Q5, 2026-10-31): wording from PROFESSOR-REVIEW-TRIAGE-2026-10-31.md; no number.
The sum of one-pass conditionals is a factorised surrogate for the joint log-likelihood ratio; an exact ordered likelihood was not computed.
Image-text verification accuracy is the mean of $Y$ over the items of a test set, and a net accuracy change is the accuracy of the rule
under test minus that of \eqref{eq:sum} on the same items, so a positive value favours the rule under test.
The caption-visible passes are the only inputs of $\Delta_j$.
Two scores are computed with the caption masked: the position marginal $D_j=\log p_\theta(\tilde v_j\mid\text{all tokens masked})$,
and $B_j$, the log-probability of $\tilde v_j$ when the tokens of its group in a random partition of the image positions are masked and
the rest of the image is visible.
\Cref{eq:delta,eq:sum} are the whole measurement of the unweighted rule; \cref{fig:measurement} draws it.

\subsection{Corruptions}\label{sec:setting-corruptions}
Three corruptions act on the pixels before tokenisation.
They are JPEG compression at quality~10, Gaussian noise with standard deviation $\sigma=0.1$ on pixels scaled to $[0,1]$ (clipped and
rounded to 8 bits), and a $128\times128$-pixel splice, in which a region of a donor COCO image is copied to a uniformly random location.
The noise draw, the donor image, and the splice location derive from the item, so each image has one corruption realisation.
\subsection{Data and Fresh Images}\label{sec:setting-data}
Images and captions come from the COCO val2017 split~\cite{lin2014coco}.
An item is one image with its true caption and one wrong caption, the caption of another image; \cref{sec:study2-protocol} states how the
wrong caption is drawn.
Test images are fresh in a defined sense: they are disjoint, by COCO image id, from every image that was used to fit or design the rule.
The test fold also excludes every image used anywhere in the project, including the earlier preregistered stage that supplied the design
images (\cref{sec:study2-provenance}), and every SugarCrepe image~\cite{hsieh2023sugarcrepe} that the project had prepared.

\subsection{Tests, Multiplicity, and Preregistration}\label{sec:setting-tests}
Every test compares two decision rules on the same $n$ items.
Let $b$ be the number of items that the rule under test decides correctly and the reference rule wrongly, and $c$ the reverse.
The net accuracy change is $(b-c)/n$, and the exact McNemar test~\cite{mcnemar1947} conditions on the $n_d=b+c$ discordant items:
\begin{align}
p_{\rm one}&=\Pr\big[\mathrm{Bin}(n_d,\tfrac12)\ge b\big],\notag\\
p_{\rm two}&=\min\big\{1,\ 2\min\big(p_{\rm one},\,\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n_d,\tfrac12)\le b]\big)\big\}.
\label{eq:mcnemar}
\end{align}
The one-sided form of \eqref{eq:mcnemar} is used only for hypotheses with a preregistered direction, and every other test is two-sided.
Within a family of hypotheses, the familywise error rate is held at 0.05 by Holm's step-down procedure~\cite{holm1979sequentially}.
Intervals for the net accuracy change are paired percentile bootstrap intervals over items.
