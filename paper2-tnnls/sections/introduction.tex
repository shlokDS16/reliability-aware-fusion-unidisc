\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{} unified masked diffusion model places text tokens and image tokens in one sequence and is trained to predict any
masked token from any visible subset, the mask token standing for ``unknown''~\cite{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm,ou2025radd}.
Such models generate and understand both modalities with one network~\cite{swerdlow2025unidisc,yang2025mmada,li2026omnidiffusion}.
With the image tokens masked and a caption visible, the same network assigns a probability to each observed image code, with no
external encoder involved.
In this paper, \emph{image-text verification} means this image-direction decision: for one image and two candidate captions, the true
caption is chosen if the observed image tokens are, summed over tokens, more probable under it than under a wrong caption
(\cref{sec:setting}).

Generative models are already used as scorers in this way.
Diffusion classifiers compare conditional denoising errors across labels~\cite{li2023diffusionclassifier,clark2023zeroshot}, image-text
matching has been scored by a conditional error normalised by the unconditional one~\cite{krojer2023diffusionitm}, and the authors of
UniDisc give examples in which damaged image-caption pairs are repaired by masking, re-infilling, and selection by the model's own
likelihood~\cite{swerdlow2025unidisc}.
This paper studies evidence that the unified model itself computes, so its behaviour is a property of the scorer, not of an attached
encoder.
Images can be damaged before a model sees them, by compression, sensor noise, or editing; corruption benchmarks treat such damage as a test
condition~\cite{hendrycks2019benchmarking}, and multimodal fusion methods are evaluated under it~\cite{zhang2024informative}.
The problem studied here is how the per-token likelihood evidence of a unified masked diffusion model behaves for image-text verification
when the image is damaged before tokenisation, and whether a fixed rule applied to that evidence changes the decision
(\cref{fig:measurement}).


Two questions about this evidence were preregistered separately (\cref{sec:setting-tests}), each with its own plan, test images, and decision
rule.

\noindent\textbf{Q1} (Study~1, \cref{sec:study1}): does discarding the image tokens that the model's own masked likelihood flags as
improbable, with a flag threshold set by conformal risk control on clean pairs, change image-direction verification accuracy under Joint
Photographic Experts Group (JPEG) compression, Gaussian noise, and a pixel splice?

\noindent\textbf{Q2} (Study~2, \cref{sec:study2}): does one frozen, learned per-token weighting of the evidence raise image-direction
verification accuracy on fresh images under Gaussian noise?

\paragraph*{Contributions}
Each item was delivered regardless of the outcome; items 1 and 2 belong to Study~1, and item 3 to Study~2.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A measured gate and a closed-form condition.} We specify, and treat as the object of measurement, an input-level
source-validation gate for a pretrained unified masked diffusion model: each image token is scored by the model's own masked likelihood
with the caption withheld, and the flag threshold is set by conformal risk control on clean pairs so that the expected flagged share of a
clean pair's tokens is at most $\alpha$.
Under an independent-token approximation, we give the condition on the false-alarm and hit rates under which discarding flagged tokens
raises the signal-to-noise ratio of an additive evidence sum.
The condition is stated as an approximation because image tokens are dependent, and it is extended to exchangeably correlated tokens
(\cref{prop:snrcorr}); it is linked to no result (\cref{sec:study1-method}).
\item \emph{The preregistered test and its checks} (exact McNemar, Holm over three realistic corruptions, and $n$ fixed by a headroom rule).

The preregistered test excludes an improvement or a loss of more than five percentage points in image-direction verification accuracy
from gating under JPEG quality~10, Gaussian noise with $\sigma=0.1$, and a 128-pixel splice.

In the preregistered comparison it is below a texture score whose sign was chosen on the same items, so the preregistered falsifier for
the score was met (\cref{fig:detection}).
We check the clean false-alarm statement on held-out clean test pairs.
Re-infilling of the flagged tokens and the score-level combination of the two directions on foil captions were run as formal tests.

\paragraph*{Scope of this paper}
This paper reports two preregistered measurements on one model and does not run a race between verifiers.
Its object is the evidence that a unified masked diffusion model computes about its own inputs, and each question asks, by an exact
paired test, whether a rule fixed in advance changes the decision on images the rule never saw.
The paper does not claim a verifier that ranks with dedicated encoders; any result for another model, dataset, severity, or kind of
negative caption; that the learned weights track reliability or corruption; or that either study bears on the other.
