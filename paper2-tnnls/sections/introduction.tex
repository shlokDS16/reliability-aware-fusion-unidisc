\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\IEEEPARstart{A}{} unified masked diffusion model places text tokens and image tokens in one sequence and is trained to predict any
masked token from any visible subset, the mask token standing for ``unknown''~\cite{austin2021d3pm,sahoo2024mdlm,ou2025radd}.
Such models generate and understand both modalities with one network~\cite{swerdlow2025unidisc,yang2025mmada,li2026omnidiffusion}.
With the image tokens masked and a caption visible, the same network assigns a probability to each observed image code, with no
external encoder involved.
In this paper, \emph{image-text verification} means this image-direction decision: for one image and two candidate captions, the true
caption is chosen if the observed image tokens are, summed over tokens, more probable under it than under a wrong caption
(\cref{sec:setting}).

Generative models are already used as scorers in this way.
Diffusion classifiers compare conditional denoising errors across labels~\cite{li2023diffusionclassifier,clark2023zeroshot}, image-text
matching has been scored by a conditional error normalised by the unconditional one~\cite{krojer2023diffusionitm}, and the authors of
UniDisc give examples in which damaged image-caption pairs are repaired by masking, re-infilling, and selection by the model's own
likelihood~\cite{swerdlow2025unidisc}.
This paper studies evidence that the unified model itself computes, so its behaviour is a property of the scorer, not of an attached
encoder.
Images can be damaged before a model sees them, by compression, sensor noise, or editing; corruption benchmarks treat such damage as a test
condition~\cite{hendrycks2019benchmarking}, and multimodal fusion methods are evaluated under it~\cite{zhang2024informative}.

\paragraph*{Problem}
The per-token evidence is a sum of terms of unequal quality.
A damaged image changes some image tokens and leaves others intact, and the model's evidence at a changed token need not point toward
the true caption.
When evidence comes from sources of unequal quality, the classical combination rule gives each source a weight that reflects its
reliability~\cite{chair1986optimal,kittler1998combining}; here each image token is such a source, and the unweighted sum gives all of them
the same weight.
The problem studied here is whether a per-token weighting of the likelihood evidence of a frozen unified masked diffusion model, fitted
in advance and then fixed, raises image-text verification accuracy on fresh images when the image is damaged before tokenisation
(\cref{fig:measurement}).
The question was preregistered with its own plan, test images, and decision rule (\cref{sec:setting-tests}): does one frozen, learned
per-token weighting of the evidence raise image-direction verification accuracy on fresh images under Gaussian noise?

\paragraph*{Scope of this paper}
This paper reports one preregistered measurement on one model and does not run a race between verifiers.
Its object is the evidence that a unified masked diffusion model computes about its own inputs, and its question is whether a rule fixed in
advance changes the decision, by an exact paired test, on images the rule never saw.
The paper does not claim a verifier that ranks with dedicated encoders, or any result for another model, dataset, severity, or kind of
negative caption; it does not claim that the learned weights track reliability or corruption.
